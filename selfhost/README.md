# A desk on your own computer

You can run a desk on a computer of your own, instead of in the cloud, and use it from your office like any other desk. It runs the same desk software, with the same Browser and apps. What you do on it goes straight between your browser and that computer, end to end, and never through us.

This is an early version for technical members, and it's open on our test platform only for now.

## What changes when the desk is yours

- **Your data stays on your computer.** We keep no copy, take no snapshots and make no backups of it. Back up the computer yourself.
- **You keep it running.** It's online in your office while the computer is on, and offline when it's off. We can't start it for you, and it never goes to sleep on its own.
- **You keep it safe.** It installs security updates for its system by itself. Keep the computer it runs on updated, and encrypt its disks if you can.
- **What we see:** that it's online, the internet address it connects from, its software version, and the address your browser uses to reach it while a connection starts. Never what's on it.
- **We can only disconnect it.** It runs software we provide, so the honest limit in our Privacy Policy applies to it too.

## What it needs

- **A computer with an x86-64 processor** (most desktops, mini PCs and home servers) that can run virtual machines, such as Proxmox, libvirt or VirtualBox.
- **A virtual machine for the desk alone,** with:
  - 2 processor cores and 4 GB of memory, or more for a smoother picture;
  - a system disk of at least 12 GB;
  - **a second, empty disk** of at least 16 GB for the desk's data (your Browser's profile, downloads and notes). Use a SCSI or SATA virtual disk, not VirtIO Block. The desk formats it the first time, and never again;
  - its network **bridged** onto your home network, so other devices can reach it. A "shared" or NAT network hides it.
- **Debian 13** on the virtual machine, freshly installed. The minimal "cloud" image works, as does the regular installer with no desktop.
- An internet connection with about 10 Mbps of upload if you'll use it away from home.

Give the desk its own virtual machine, not your own computer's system: it keeps the desk apart from everything else you have.

## Install

1. **Make the virtual machine** with the two disks above, and install Debian 13 on the system disk. Leave the second disk empty.
2. **Copy the installer onto it.** Download it from the `/self-hosted` page (`ma-selfhost-<version>.tar.gz`) and unpack it with `tar -xzf`: it's a folder with `selfhost/`, `image/recipe/` and a release bundle (`bundle/`), from Minimalist Alliance. Before it changes anything, the installer checks that the bundle and its own files are exactly the ones Minimalist Alliance signed, and stops if they aren't. To check it yourself as well, see "Checking the installer yourself" below.
3. **Run it,** as root:
   ```sh
   sudo ./selfhost/install.sh --bundle ./bundle
   ```
   It takes 10 to 20 minutes, mostly downloading. It installs the desk's software, sets up the second disk and the firewall, and then shows a code:
   ```
     Your code:  ABCD-EFGH

     On a computer where you're signed in, open
     https://platform.staging.minimalistalliance.com/self-hosted?code=ABCDEFGH
     and add this desk to your office. The code works for 15 minutes.
   ```
4. **Open that link** on your laptop, signed in to your account. Check the code, name the desk, and choose **Add to my office**. Only use a code from a computer you set up yourself.
5. Back at the computer, the installer shows whose office the desk just joined, with the name and address partly hidden, and asks **Is that your account? Keep this desk there? [y/N]**. Answer `y`. On `n` (or Enter) the desk is taken out of that office and forgets the pairing, so a code someone else gave you can't leave a desk of theirs in your office. `--yes` answers it for you, for a script. On a data disk from a desk that has run before, the installer also says, before the code and again here, that **whoever's office this desk joins gets that Browser, signed in** (below, "Removing it").
6. The installer says **Paired**, starts the desk, and it appears in your office. Open it there like any other desk.

Options for `install.sh`:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| `--address <ip>` | The address your browser reaches the desk on. Leave it out to use the desk's address on your home network. Set a public address only if you've forwarded the desk's port (below) to it |
| `--port <n>` | The port your browser reaches the desk on, UDP and TCP. 4443 unless you choose another, from 1024 up: Chrome won't connect to a desk on your home network on a lower one. `sudo ma-selfhost port <n>` changes it later |
| `--keep-ssh` | Let SSH in from your home network. Without it, the desk's firewall lets in only its own port. `--no-keep-ssh` closes it again |
| `--allow-home-network` | Let the desk's Browser reach the other devices on your home network. Without it, a web page open on the desk can't reach your router, printer or other devices, over IPv4 or IPv6, and the desk keeps up as your network changes (if it ever can't, its Browser uses IPv4 alone until it can). `--no-allow-home-network` undoes it |
| `--yes` | Answer pairing's questions yes: whether the office shown is yours, and, on a data disk from a desk that has run before, whether to pair it (the warning still shows). For scripts |
| `--no-pair` | Don't wait here for the code. The desk still asks for one; `sudo ma-selfhost pair --wait` shows it |

When you update the desk, the installer keeps the desk's options, so you only give the ones you want to change.

## Using it at home and away

- **At home,** your browser connects straight to the desk over your home network. That's the fastest way to use it.
- **If Chrome asks** whether the office may "find devices on your local network", allow it. On a Mac, Chrome and Firefox also need **Local Network** turned on for them in System Settings, under Privacy & Security.
- **Away from home,** your browser has to reach the desk some other way. Either join your home network with a VPN you already use (WireGuard, Tailscale and the like), and install with that VPN's address of the desk in `--address`; or forward the desk's port (4443 unless you chose another, UDP and TCP, to the same port) on your router to the desk and give your public address. We don't relay desks on your own computer yet.
- Guest Wi-Fi and some work networks keep devices apart. There, use the VPN.

## Day to day

```sh
sudo ma-selfhost status        # which desk this is, whether it's online, and its port
sudo ma-selfhost port 5000     # stream on another port (1024 up); the firewall follows
journalctl -u deskd -u desk-rtc -u desk-notebook -u ma-selfhost    # the desk's own logs
```

- **Updating:** copy the newer installer and bundle onto the desk, as when you installed it, and run `sudo ./selfhost/install.sh --bundle ./bundle` again. It takes only a bundle signed with the key your desk already has, and changes nothing otherwise. To check the new folder with your desk's own checker before you run anything from it: `sudo ma-selfhost verify ./folder` (the folder that holds `selfhost/`, `image/` and `bundle/`). It says which versions it's updating from and which settings it keeps: the platform, address, port, SSH and home network. It gives each part a quick health check as it goes in, and puts the old one back if it fails. Your data, your Browser's sign-ins and the pairing stay, and the desk stays in your office. A desk window you have open drops for a moment while its parts restart.
- **One copy at a time:** a desk can run on one computer at a time. If you start a copy (a cloned virtual machine, say) while the first is online, Minimalist Alliance refuses the second. `sudo ma-selfhost status` and the installer then say, in plain words, that the desk is already running somewhere else, and the copy starts by itself about 3 minutes after the first stops. Your office says "<desk name> started at <time>" for each new start, and "Another copy of <desk name> is trying to start" while a second one is being turned away, so a start you didn't make is one you notice. If that happens, remove the desk from your office and pair it again.
- **Restarting:** restart the virtual machine at any time. The desk comes back online by itself.
- **Moving it:** the pairing and everything in the desk live on the second disk, so a fresh system disk with the same data disk carries on as the same desk.

## Checking the installer yourself

Every installer and bundle from Minimalist Alliance is signed with one key, the self-hosted release key, which signs nothing else, and the installer won't install anything that key didn't sign. On a first install the key it checks with comes in the same folder, so a folder someone changed could carry a key of theirs. If the folder didn't come straight from us, check that key before you run anything, from the folder:

```sh
sha256sum selfhost/files/opt/ma/etc/selfhost-release-keys/ma-selfhost-release-2026-11.pem
openssl dgst -sha256 -verify selfhost/files/opt/ma/etc/selfhost-release-keys/ma-selfhost-release-2026-11.pem \
  -signature bundle/bundle.pin.sig bundle/bundle.pin
sha256sum -c bundle/installer.sha256
```

The first line must print the release key's sha256:

```
a334decc5565bfef53d704e30f11041b5e71b499d8677c779ec8a3c2f7024633
```

Compare it with the copies we publish apart from your folder, not only with this guide inside it: the `/self-hosted` page, where you download the installer, shows the same number beside the download ("check this matches what the installer prints"), and so does this guide in the public copy of this repository (`github.com/minimalist-alliance/ma-office`, `selfhost/README.md`). If any of them differ, don't run the installer. The second command must say `Verified OK`, and the third `OK` for every file. The installer prints the same key's sha256 when it starts (`signed by ma-selfhost-release-2026-11.pem, key sha256 a334decc…`). After the first install your desk keeps that key in `/opt/ma/etc/selfhost-release-keys/`, and an update signed with any other is refused. The folder also carries the key desks take update batches from (`image/recipe/files/opt/ma/etc/batch-keys/`): that one never vouches for an installer, so it isn't the one to compare.

## A teen's desk

If your account is a teen's, the desk's Browser runs under the same extra rules as a cloud desk: a family filter on web addresses, SafeSearch, YouTube's restricted mode, no proxies and no extensions. **Whoever owns the computer has root on it and can remove those rules,** by deleting a file. We can't stop that, and we don't try: a self-hosted desk browses from your own network, where your family's own filters still apply.

## Removing it

Remove it from your office on the same page you added it on (`/self-hosted`). It stops connecting at once, and everything on it stays on your computer, the Browser's sign-ins included. To use the computer as a desk again later:

```sh
sudo ma-selfhost forget && sudo systemctl restart ma-selfhost && sudo ma-selfhost pair --wait
```

**The Browser goes with the desk.** Its profile, with whatever it's signed in to, stays on the data disk through `forget`, a new pairing and a reinstall. So once the desk has run (its Browser starts with it), `forget` and `pair` first say that whoever's office this desk joins gets that Browser, signed in, and ask **[y/N]**: only `y` goes on. Confirming whose office it joined says it again. If someone else will use this computer as a desk, sign out of your sites in its Browser before you remove it from your office. With no terminal to ask at (a script), `forget` and `pair` refuse unless given `--yes`, which still prints the warning.

If you think someone copied your desk's disk, remove it from your office straight away, then pair it again. To replace its key without removing it: `sudo ma-selfhost rotate`.

## For the team: how it works

Decision and security write-up: `ma-platform` `docs/decisions/draft-self-hosted-desks.md` (platform) and `../docs/decisions/draft-self-hosted-desk.md` (this side). Contract: `ma-platform` `docs/contracts/selfhost.md`. Status: `../docs/sub-status/selfhost-notebook.md` and the other `selfhost-*` files there.

**Pairing and identity.** `ma-selfhost` (`files/usr/local/lib/ma/selfhost/ma-selfhost`, Python 3 with `python3-cryptography`) makes an Ed25519 key on `/data/selfhost/key.pem` (0600, user `selfhost`, uid 1110), asks the platform for a code, and once a member types it, trades signed requests for 15-minute credentials of the env's self-hosted desk role. It serves them to deskd exactly where a cloud desk's metadata service would: IMDSv2 on `169.254.169.254:80`, an address it puts on `lo`. The existing firewall rule already lets only root and deskd (1100) reach that address. So **deskd, `desk-rtc` and `desk-stream` run unchanged,** from the same commits as the cloud releases, built for amd64. What it serves: the tags `DeskId`, `UserId`, `DeskKind`, `Env`; `instance-id` (the desk ID, which the platform expects for these desks); `public-ipv4` (the desk's address, see below); `instance-type` `self-hosted`; and the credentials under `iam/security-credentials/`. It never logs the key or a credential, and `log_message` is silenced so tokens and paths don't reach the journal.

**The DTLS identity is signed by the key.** The platform takes this desk's hello only with DTLS fingerprints its key signed (`ma-platform` contract `selfhost.md` §3 and §4), so someone holding a copied 15-minute credential can't point the member's sessions at another machine. desk-rtc keeps its identity in one file with its private key (`/data/rtc`, `0600`, user rtc), which `ma-selfhost` can't read, so `ma-selfhost-dtls`, as root, reads only the certificate's block and writes its sha-256 to `/run/ma-selfhost-dtls/dtls.json`: at `ma-selfhost`'s start (its prep) and after each desk-rtc start (the drop-in `desk-rtc.service.d/selfhost.conf`, once desk-rtc listens). That directory is root's alone, not `ma-selfhost`'s own `/run/ma-selfhost`, and desk-rtc's file is read without following a link (below, "Root and the services' folders"). `ma-selfhost` watches that file and signs it into its next `session` call at once. `ma-selfhost status` shows `identity: signed`, `waiting` (desk-rtc has none yet), or `unsupported` (a platform from before signed statements). `install.sh` waits for it before starting deskd, which otherwise tries hello again a minute later.

**Root and the services' folders (EXP-7).** Root never opens, writes, `chown`s or `chmod`s a name inside a directory a service's user owns, since that user could put a link there and have root act on whatever it names. Where the thing is the service's, its own user does it (`setpriv --no-new-privs`); where root has to read a service's file, it opens the directory and then the file with `O_NOFOLLOW`, and takes only a regular file; root's own output goes to a directory only root can write. Every root step on a self-hosted desk was checked (`../docs/sub-status/selfhost-exp.md`, "Root writes checked"). The ones that changed:
- `ma-selfhost-dtls` writes to `/run/ma-selfhost-dtls/` (above), and reads `/data/rtc/dtls-identity.pem` (user `rtc`'s) with `O_NOFOLLOW`, so a link or a FIFO there reads as "no identity yet".
- `desk-datadirs` (the recipe's, every boot; cloud desks too, from their next bake) makes `/data/browser/{profile,downloads,home}` as `kiosk`, and copies the volume record into `/data/desk` as `desk`.
- `install.sh` builds GStreamer's plugin registry with `HOME=/run/desk-stream` (the value desk-stream runs with, so nothing rescans) but in a mount namespace of its own with an empty `/run`, so it never loads a plugin from user `stream`'s real `/run/desk-stream`. It reads `ma-selfhost`'s `status.json` as `selfhost`.
- `desk-browser-close` (the recipe's) gives up after 5 s on a profile file that doesn't read, so a FIFO there can't hold the Browser's stop.

**The home network (EXP-9).** On a home network with IPv6 the router, printer and phones have global addresses as well as private ones, and some homes have a LAN on public IPv4, so refusing the Browser the private ranges doesn't keep it off them. The firewall has two sets more, `home4` and `home6`: every prefix the kernel routes straight onto one of the desk's own links (no gateway), and every route a router advertisement announced (another segment the router serves), but never a default route, loopback, or a prefix shorter than /8 (IPv4) or /16 (IPv6), which is how a VPN takes over the whole internet. The Browser (1102) is refused both, tagged `#HOME` so `--allow-home-network` still lifts it, and `desk-agent-filter`'s (1106) TCP 443 leaves both out. `ma-selfhost-net` (`files/usr/local/lib/ma/selfhost/`, root, `ma-selfhost-net.service`) fills them when it starts and again within about a second of any route change (`ip monitor route`), so a new prefix from the router, a VPN coming up or the desk moving to another network is covered without a restart. It writes `/run/ma-selfhost-net/home.nft`, which `nftables.conf` includes after its rules, so a reload or restart of the firewall loads the latest list in the same transaction as the rules; it's a pattern, so a boot before the first fill still loads. `desk-browser.service` waits for its first fill (`desk-browser.service.d/selfhost.conf`, `Wants=` so a broken helper never stops the Browser). The desk's own traffic (root, `deskd`, `desk-rtc`) isn't affected. `sudo /usr/local/lib/ma/selfhost/ma-selfhost-net show` lists them. Tests: `tests/test_selfhost_net.py`, and `tests/firewall.sh` with real connections in a throwaway namespace.

**It fails closed (Nov 11).** If `ma-selfhost-net` stopped, hung, or couldn't read or load the routes, `home6` would go stale while the network changed. So each fill that loads (at start, after a route change, on a firewall reload's HUP, and every minute anyway) also puts the Browser (1102) and `desk-agent-filter` (1106) in a third set, `ipv6_ok`, in the same transaction, and the set gives them a 3-minute timeout. The Browser gets IPv6 beyond loopback only while it's in that set (two `#HOME` rules: a reset for a new connection, so Chromium goes to the site's IPv4 address at once, and the ICMPv6 refusal for the rest), and so does `desk-agent-filter`'s TCP 443 (it tries IPv4 first anyway). `home4` has no timeout and keeps its last list, so IPv4 to the internet works as before. `home.nft` never carries `ipv6_ok`, so a reload or a boot loads it empty and IPv6 stays closed until the helper's next fill, moments later. The helper restarts for as long as it takes (`Restart=always`, `StartLimitIntervalSec=0`, back-off to a minute). A desk installed with `--allow-home-network` has neither rule, so its Browser's IPv6 never depends on it. To see it: `sudo nft list set inet ma ipv6_ok` (two elements with what's left of their 3 minutes) and `systemctl status ma-selfhost-net`.

**Pairing on a shared machine (Security's EXP-8).** `/run/ma-selfhost/status.json` holds the live pairing code, so it's `0640` `selfhost:selfhost`, in a directory systemd makes `0750` (`RuntimeDirectoryMode`): another account on the machine can't read the code and pair the desk into its own office. `write_json` writes every file from a fresh temp file of its own (`mkstemp`), set to the final mode before a byte goes in, then renamed, so none is ever wider than its mode, whatever the umask or an older file was. Its readers are all `selfhost` or root: `ma-selfhost status` and `pair` (as `selfhost`, through the wrapper), `install.sh` (as `selfhost` since EXP-7, waiting for the file and for `identity`) and `e2e/` (`sudo cat`); root writes nothing there (`ma-selfhost-dtls` writes `/run/ma-selfhost-dtls/dtls.json`, EXP-7). **The Browser warning:** only root can see `/data/browser` (the service has `InaccessiblePaths=-/data/browser`), so the root wrapper `/usr/local/sbin/ma-selfhost` checks whether `/data/browser/profile`, Chromium's user data directory, has its `Local State` or `Default`: two `test`s, nothing under it opened, listed, written or changed (ADR 0035). It passes the answer as `MA_SELFHOST_BROWSER=profile` or `none`, set every time so a caller's value never counts; run any other way, `ma-selfhost` assumes a profile. `pair` asks before it shows a code and says it again beside the account it confirms; `forget` asks first; the decline path's own forget doesn't ask twice. Tests: `tests/test_selfhost.py`, `tests/browser_profile.sh`.

**Nobody traces the pairing client (Security's `ptrace` note, Nov 11).** `sudo ma-selfhost pair --wait` runs the client as `selfhost` on the admin's terminal, and the kernel (`kernel.yama.ptrace_scope = 0` on Debian) lets any other process of that user trace it, so a compromised `ma-selfhost serve` could have watched or faked what the admin sees at the y/N question (not typed into their shell: `dev.tty.legacy_tiocsti = 0`). Now `ma-selfhost.service`, the only long-running process of that user, has `SystemCallFilter=~@debug` (no `ptrace`, `pidfd_getfd` or `perf_event_open`) and `SystemCallArchitectures=native`, and the client makes itself non-dumpable (`prctl(PR_SET_DUMPABLE, 0)`) as its first step, before its other imports and before it reads anything, which also gives its `/proc` files (`mem`, `fd`, `environ`) to root. Every command of the client does it, the service included. **Left open:** the interpreter's start (about 10 ms) before that step, in which another process of the user could still open its `/proc/<pid>/mem` and keep it (`../docs/sub-status/selfhost-exp.md`, "Nov 11 follow-up"). Tests: `tests/test_selfhost.py`, `NobodyTracesThePairingClient` (any unit that runs as `selfhost` must refuse `@debug`).

**The address.** `desk-rtc` binds the default route's source address (`--bind auto`), and uses `configure`'s `publicIpv4` as its one host candidate. `ma-selfhost` serves that same source address as `public-ipv4`, or the `address=` in `/etc/ma/selfhost.conf`. So the candidate is the desk's home-network address: the contract's exception for these desks (`selfhost.md` §4). One candidate means one path; offering home, VPN and forwarded addresses together needs a `desk-rtc` change (STATUS.md).

**The port.** Chrome (156) drops a remote ICE host candidate with a private address on a port below 1024 (its log says "Invalid candidate"), so a desk on a home network can't use 443: ICE never starts, and the office shows a session that never connects. Measured on the first run here, with the same address on port 5000 and a public address on 443 both accepted, and nothing about Local Network Access changing it. So a self-hosted desk listens on 4443. **`desk-rtc` takes its port from the desk's config** (task 35): `port=4443` in `/etc/ma/desk-rtc.conf`, which its release's `run` passes as `--config`, and its `health` asks `desk-rtc --print-port` which port to check, so the two can't differ. A cloud desk has no such file and listens on 443 as before. A value it can't use stops `desk-rtc` and fails `health`; a key it doesn't know is skipped. **One helper sets it,** `ma-selfhost-port` (`files/usr/local/lib/ma/selfhost/`), for both `install.sh --port` and `sudo ma-selfhost port <n>`: the config and the firewall's `rtc_port`, checked with `nft -c` first, then the firewall reloaded and `desk-rtc` restarted (its DTLS identity stays on `/data/rtc`). The bundle's `desk-rtc` is the cloud desks' release built for amd64, with nothing patched.

**Desks from the first bundle** (`desk-rtc` `0.3.0-sh1`, task 31) had `run` and `health` patched to read `/etc/ma/desk-rtc-port`. Updating one with `install.sh` reads its settings from what it has (the port from that file, SSH and the home network from which tagged lines its firewall kept), writes `/etc/ma/desk-rtc.conf`, keeps the old file in step while `0.3.0-sh1` is still installed (so if `ma-update` puts it back after a failed health check, it still listens on 4443), and removes the old file once the new `desk-rtc` is in. Rolling a desk back by hand to `0.3.0-sh1` later: `sudo /opt/ma/bin/ma-update desk-rtc --rollback && sudo ma-selfhost port 4443`, which rewrites the old file for it.

**After a restart the office waits up to 3 minutes.** The platform takes one live boot per self-hosted desk (`ma-platform` contract `selfhost.md` §4, 0.3.43), and a restart is a new boot, so `deskd`'s hello is refused (`409 boot_in_use`) until the last boot's last heartbeat is 3 minutes old. Measured on the S-85 stand-in: the machine back in 21 to 23 s, `ma-selfhost` online and the smoke test passing at 29 to 30 s, three hellos refused, and the hello accepted at 206 s (`deskd` tries once a minute, not at the 30 s `Retry-After`). The member's "comes back online by itself" holds; it just isn't at once. `selfhost/e2e` waits for the hello.

**The teen lock here.** `install.sh`'s "install files" step copies `etc/ma/chromium-teen.json`, `usr/local/lib/ma/desk-browser-policy` and `desk-browser.service` with the rest of the recipe. Debian's amd64 `chromium` reads the same `/etc/chromium/policies/managed/`. Proven on a Debian 13 amd64 box, with `install.sh`'s package line and file steps as written, its firewall and a second disk: the real unit locked and unlocked, Chromium reported all 7 keys, uid 1102 reached the resolver, the filtered name didn't load and `example.net` did (`../image/evidence/teen-lock-2026-11-07/amd64.txt`). `install.sh` itself wasn't run end to end, since it needs a bundle and a pairing. **A bundle's `deskd` has to write the band.** A missing band is locked, so a bundle with a `deskd` that doesn't filters an adult's Browser too: move `build-bundle.sh`'s `deskd` pin with the Desk Engineer's release before a member updates. A self-hosted desk's `/etc/ma-release` has no image tag, so the Platform can't gate it on `DeskTeenLock`; an older installer simply has no lock.

**Files here:** `install.sh`; `build-bundle.sh` (the amd64 releases, built on a Docker host, never on the desk or the hypervisor); `pin-release-key.sh` and `release-keys.sha256` (the release key's pin and anchor, "A signed bundle"); `files/` (installed over the recipe's: `ma-selfhost.service`, its prep, `ma-selfhost-dtls`, `ma-selfhost-port` and CLI wrapper, `ma-selfhost-net` and its unit, deskd's, desk-rtc's and the Browser's drop-ins, `nftables.conf`, `chrony.conf`); `tests/` (`python3 -m unittest discover -s selfhost/tests`, no root; `root_links.sh` and `firewall.sh` need root in a throwaway container, their headers say how); `e2e/` (the end-to-end proof against staging, `e2e/README.md`); `lab/standin.sh` (a temporary amd64 EC2 machine standing in for a member's, for that proof when the owner's Proxmox server isn't free; its few test-only differences are in its header).

**A signed bundle (EXP-10).** Whoever could change the folder a member installs from could once change a release and its line in `bundle.pin` together, and `install.sh`, as root, installed it; the installer's own files and the batch baseline weren't checked at all, and an update even replaced the desk's batch key with the folder's. Now:
- **What's signed:** `bundle.pin`, whose first line is `# ma-selfhost-bundle/1`. Besides each release's sha256 it names `installer.sha256` (sha256sum's format: every file of `selfhost/`, less `e2e/`, `lab/` and `tests/`, and of `image/recipe/`, as git has them at the bundle's commit) and, with a baseline, `batch-baseline`. The signature is `bundle.pin.sig`: ECDSA P-256 SHA-256 over the file's exact bytes, DER, at most 4096 bytes signed, the same form as an update batch.
- **The key** is the self-hosted release key, KMS `alias/ma-selfhost-release` (`ECC_NIST_P256`, `ECDSA_SHA_256` only), the public channel's own (from Nov 11; before, bundles were signed with the update batches' `alias/ma-desk-batch`). It never signs an update batch, and the batch key never vouches for a bundle: `ma-selfhost-bundle` never reads `batch-keys/`, and `desk-batch` trusts only `batch-keys/` (below, "Signed updates"). Its public half is the installer's `files/opt/ma/etc/selfhost-release-keys/ma-selfhost-release-2026-11.pem` (sha256 `a334decc…4633`), alone in its folder, installed as `/opt/ma/etc/selfhost-release-keys/` (root's, the folder `0755`, the file `0644`). **Its anchor** is the committed `release-keys.sha256`, one line `<sha256>  <name>.pem` (sha256sum's format): the checker trusts the folder only when it holds exactly that file with that sha256, a P-256 key, and nothing else. **Pinning it is one step:** `selfhost/pin-release-key.sh alias/ma-selfhost-release` reads the public half with KMS `GetPublicKey`, refuses anything but `ECC_NIST_P256` with `ECDSA_SHA_256` alone, writes the PEM as `openssl pkey -pubin -inform DER -outform PEM` writes it from KMS's DER (byte for byte what Platform's `scripts/selfhost-publish.sh` publishes as `ma-selfhost-release-key.pem`) and the anchor, and runs the installer's own check of them; the same key again changes nothing, another needs `--replace`. Commit both files together. How the two kinds of signature are kept apart: `../docs/decisions/draft-self-hosted-desk.md`, point 8.
- **Signing:** `selfhost/build-bundle.sh --sign <outdir>`, with credentials the key's policy allows `kms:Sign` (`BUNDLE_KMS_KEY_ID` defaults to `alias/ma-selfhost-release`; set for the build, it also signs at its end). It refuses `alias/ma-desk-batch`, a bundle from a clone with changes, a commit whose release key doesn't match its anchor, an `installer.sha256` that isn't its commit's, files that don't match `bundle.pin`, and a release with no `passed-test.json` (`desk-agent-filter`, which isn't published, is built from its pinned source), and it checks the signature with the installer's own check before it keeps it. A signature is the go-ahead for anyone who gets the bundle, so sign a bundle you'll prove on a test machine next, and publish only one that passed there.
- **The check** is `files/usr/local/lib/ma/selfhost/ma-selfhost-bundle`, which `install.sh` runs before its first change (it installs `python3` and `openssl` from Debian first if the machine lacks them). The installer's release key must match its anchor (`ma-selfhost-bundle keys <folder>` runs only that part and prints the key's sha256). Every release must hash to its line, `installer.sha256` to its line, every file it lists to its own line, with no other file anywhere in `selfhost/files/` or `image/recipe/` (which `install.sh` copies wholesale), and the baseline to its line. Nothing is read through a link.
- **A first install** checks against the release key in the installer, after its anchor, and prints its sha256. That proves the bundle and the installer go together, not who made them: `install.sh` itself runs before any check can, so the folder as a whole is trusted, and a member who didn't get it straight from us checks the key by hand ("Checking the installer yourself", above).
- **An update** checks against the release key the desk already holds, `/opt/ma/etc/selfhost-release-keys/`, with the desk's own installed checker, and refuses an installer whose release key folder holds any other key, the same key under another name, or anything beside it. So an update never changes the key. `sudo ma-selfhost verify <folder>` runs the same check from the desk before anything in the new folder runs. **A desk with no release key is refused**, saying why, before anything runs: one installed from a bundle the batch key signed (the internal test bundles before Nov 11; no member has one) or from before signed bundles. It was a fallback to the installer's key before; now such a desk is installed fresh. **Moving to a new release key isn't built:** it needs a signed statement from the old key naming the new one.
- **The folder a member gets** is `git archive <the bundle's commit> selfhost image/recipe` with the bundle beside it as `bundle/`, holding only `bundle.pin`, its signature, `installer.sha256`, the releases and their `.sha256` files. **Platform publishes it** (`ma-platform` `scripts/selfhost-publish.sh`, which never signs): it checks the folder against `alias/ma-selfhost-release`'s public half from KMS as a first install would, and refuses anything the signature doesn't cover, then puts `ma-selfhost-<version>.tar.gz`, its `.sha256`, the key as `ma-selfhost-release-key.pem` and `latest.json` (with the key's sha256) behind `https://platform.<env's domain>/self-hosted/download/`, which the `/self-hosted` page reads. The first release, `2026.11.11-377c648`, was built, signed and proven on an amd64 stand-in and handed to Platform (`../docs/sub-status/selfhost-release-1.md`).

**A bundle** (`build-bundle.sh`) is `bundle.pin`, `installer.sha256`, `bundle.pin.sig` once signed, and six amd64 artifacts: `desk-rtc` at the commit its arm64 release came from, with that release's own `run` and `health` (the script refuses a `desk-rtc` that doesn't read the desk's config, or a `-sh` version); `desk-stream`, byte for byte the published one (it has no arch: from 0.3.3 it carries `deskcap` for both, and its `run` picks the folder by machine; the script pins the umask the publisher packed it under, 022 for 0.3.3); `deskd` and `desk-agent` built for amd64 from `services/` as `build-image.sh` builds them; `desk-agent-filter` from its source; and `desk-notebook` built for amd64 (below). `bundle.pin`'s header names the clone's commit (`# bundle <commit>`), and with `BATCH_BASELINE=<seq>` the bundle carries `batch-baseline`, which `install.sh` puts in `/opt/ma/etc/batch-baseline` (never lowering it), so `desk-batch` refuses batches older than the bundle. Today's pins are at the top of the script. A new cloud release becomes a self-hosted one by changing the pin, building, installing it on a test machine and running `e2e/`. Go builds come out byte for byte the same each time (`desk-rtc` and `desk-agent-filter` did, twice); the Java and Node images don't, so each bundle's own `bundle.pin` is what a batch must name.

**Notebook (`desk-notebook`, S-13).** The service that keeps a desk's notes (`services/notebook/README.md`) goes on a self-hosted desk the way the image puts it on a cloud desk (`image/README.md`, "desk-notebook"):
- **The build.** `build-bundle.sh` builds it as `services/scripts/build-image.sh notebook` does, from `services/notebook/` and `web/notebook/src/model/` at its pinned commit, on the amd64 Docker host: the Dockerfile's base is `node:24-bookworm-slim` by its multi-arch digest and the service has no native code, so nothing changes for amd64. It's packed as `services/scripts/package-release.sh` packs it (`VERSION`, `run`, `health`, `desk-notebook.env`, `image.tar` of `ma/desk-notebook:<version>`), with the commit's own `run`, `health` and env file.
- **Pinned, built and proven:** `desk-notebook` and `deskd` `2026.11.07-0e1fb6c`, both passed on the test desk. On 2026-11-07 the bundle with them updated a desk from the one before and installed a new desk, and `e2e/` passed on each (`../docs/sub-status/selfhost-notebook.md`, "Proof on VMs"). The script still stops with "no desk-notebook pinned yet" if the slot is ever emptied, as it does for an unpinned `desk-rtc`: a bundle without it would leave a self-hosted desk with no Notebook.
- **The installer.** `install.sh` makes user `notebook` (uid and gid 1105, its own group only), installs the recipe's `desk-notebook.service` (enabled) and `tmpfiles.d/ma.conf` (`/run/desk-notebook`, `notebook:desk` 2750, so `deskd` reaches `notebook.sock` by group and `kiosk` and `agent` can't enter), and `desk-datadirs` makes `/data/notebook` (`notebook:notebook` 0700). It installs the release from the bundle **last**: `ma-update`'s smoke test runs every installed component's `health`, so a `desk-notebook` installed but not yet running would fail the others' updates. On a desk where it isn't running yet, it's only installed there, and starts with the desk once `/data/notebook` is there; a running one is updated through `ma-update` like the rest. `deskd`'s own `run` mounts `/run/desk-notebook`, from the `deskd` pinned with it.
- **Updating a desk from before it** adds all of that. On the data disk it only adds `/data/notebook`: it's made by `desk-datadirs`, the cloud desk's own script, and the rest of what that does in `/data` it already does at every boot.
- **Older bundles still install.** `desk-notebook` is optional in `bundle.pin`, as it is in the image's `releases.pin`, so a bundle from before it installs as it did, with Notebook's unit skipped by its condition. A bundle that has it next to a `deskd` that can't reach it is refused.
- **Nothing in it assumes the cloud.** It reads only its `DESK_NOTEBOOK_*` settings (all defaults on a desk), has no network at all, talks only over `notebook.sock`, and never asks for IMDS, AWS or a credential; its `health` is `python3` over that socket. So it needs nothing from `ma-selfhost`, and the firewall needs no rule for uid 1105.

**Signed updates (S-85).** A self-hosted desk has the whole desk half of the cloud desks' signed update batches (`image/README.md`, "Desk batches and Debian's updates"; `../docs/interfaces/desk-runtime.md` section 6.1), from `install.sh` running `build.sh`'s `desk-batch` block as written:
- **The key:** `/opt/ma/etc/batch-keys/ma-desk-batch-2026-11.pem`, the public half of KMS `alias/ma-desk-batch`, the same file as the image's; `/etc/ma/batch-keys.d/` empty. A batch signed with any other key is `err untrusted`, the self-hosted release key included: it's in its own folder, which `desk-batch` never reads.
- **The timer and socket:** `desk-batch.timer` (2 minutes after boot, then 5 after each tick) and `desk-batch.socket` on `/run/desk/batch.sock`, for `deskd`.
- **Idle:** `deskd` from `2026.11.10-7e809cb` writes `/run/desk/idle.json`. A self-hosted desk has no idle stop (`idleStopSeconds` null), so `desk-batch` wants 10 minutes with no session, Agent task or export.
- **Applying:** `ma-update --prepare` and `--activate` take amd64 artifacts as they are; nothing in them is arch-specific. A batch for a self-hosted desk says `"arch": "amd64"` and names the bundle's amd64 artifacts' sha256s (`tools/batch/batch-build --bundle <dir>`, which also holds each version to its arm64 release's `passed-test.json`). `desk-batch` refuses a batch for another machine (`err arch`; none said is arm64, as every batch before the field) and one naming an image on a desk whose `/etc/ma-release` says `hosting=self` (`err root`): there's no root volume to swap.
- **Debian's updates:** the security archive only (`51ma-unattended`), from `desk-batch`'s tick at `nice 19` and idle I/O, once a day while idle or after 72 hours with none; Debian's `apt-daily` timers are masked, as on a cloud desk. Stable point releases no longer arrive by themselves (they wait for the member's own `apt` or a new installer); a newer kernel is recorded as `reboot.pending` and waits for the member's next restart, as nothing reboots the desk.
- **Proven on an amd64 stand-in** (S-85, `../docs/sub-status/s85-selfhost-033.md`, 2026-11-11, one sample): a KMS-signed amd64 batch (`deskd` and `desk-notebook` `2026.11.10-01883dd`) offered as `deskd`'s uid, applied by the timer once `deskd`'s `idle.json` showed 10 minutes idle (prepared 8.8 s, activated 5.1 s), then `selfhost/e2e` passed on it; one signed with another key `err untrusted`, the same one again `err seq`, an arm64 one `err arch`; and Debian's security updates at idle in 20.4 s, the new kernel waiting for the next restart.
- **Not there yet, on the platform's side:** the heartbeat never names a batch to a self-hosted desk (`ma-platform` `docs/contracts/agent.md`, "Update batches"), amd64 artifacts aren't published, and `deskd` doesn't yet offer batches on any desk (the Desk Engineer's half). Until then a self-hosted desk updates its own software only by running `install.sh` again. What Platform must add is in the status doc, "For Platform".
