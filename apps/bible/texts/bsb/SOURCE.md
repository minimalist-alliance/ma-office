## Edition and publisher

- **Edition:** the current BSB text, which the publisher's downloads page labels "Current, updated to 3rd Printing". The USFM archive's files are dated 2026-03-08 (its `\id` lines say "Autogenerated BSB by bsb2usfm"); the server dates `bsb.txt` 2026-07-31.
- **Publisher:** Berean Bible (BSB Publishing), at berean.bible and bereanbible.com. Its statements are in `STATEMENT.txt`.
- **Public domain since:** April 30, 2023, by the publisher's dedication (linked to Creative Commons CC0 1.0).
- **Fetched:** 2026-11-11, only from berean.bible and bereanbible.com.

## Why two files

The publisher's USFM, USX and USJ come from the same converter and share its faults; its plain text `bsb.txt` doesn't have them. Against `bsb.txt`, the USFM's verse text differs in 1,374 of 31,086 verses:

- **1,245 only in spacing around em dashes.** The USFM has "morning — the first day" and "breath— for she was dying— she"; `bsb.txt` (and the printed BSB) has "morning—the first day" throughout. `bsb.txt` has no spaced em dash at all.
- **129 in other ways, every one a conversion fault:** a stray `[’’]` at the end of 59 verses (Exodus 20:26 "exposed on it.’[’’]"); an extra space in 50 ("“No, ” he said", "Likewise , every"); a missing space in 6 ("his sons:This"); ". . ." in 11 (Numbers 26's census totals, John 21:7); bracketed words in 2 ("[of silver]", "[it]"); and "she named vvv him Ben-oni" in Genesis 35:18.

So the words come from `bsb.txt` and the structure from the USFM. For each verse `normalize.py` takes the USFM's segments and footnote places and moves them onto `bsb.txt`'s words by aligning the two texts. **Every one of the 31,086 `V` texts is `bsb.txt`'s verse byte for byte** (after NFC and collapsing whitespace); `check.py --downloads` checks all of them. Where a poetry line ends in an em dash, `bsb.txt` runs straight into the next line ("until you return to the ground—because out of it were you taken"), and the two segments meet with no space between them: 348 such boundaries in 308 verses (Genesis 3:19, 27:28, 48:16, 49:9 and so on).

Headings and footnotes have no plain-text counterpart, so they're the USFM's own. They were searched for the same faults (stray brackets, "vvv", spaces before punctuation, run-together words, ". . ."): none found. Six footnotes keep the USFM's spaced em dashes.

## What normalizing kept and left out

Kept, all of the text, headings and footnotes:

- **Verse words:** from `bsb.txt`, as above.
- **Psalm titles inside verse 1:** in 117 psalms the publisher puts the title inside verse 1 (`\d \v 1 A Psalm of David.`, and in `bsb.txt` "Psalm 23:1 A Psalm of David. The LORD is my shepherd…"). They stay there, as the verse's first segment, of kind `d`, and their 36 footnotes ("Sheminith is probably a musical term…") are ordinary `F` lines.
- **Habakkuk 3:19's last line**, "For the choirmaster. With stringed instruments.", is a `\mr` in the USFM and part of the verse in `bsb.txt`: it's the verse's last segment, kind `d`.
- **Headings:** `\s1` and `\ms` ("BOOK I" to "BOOK V" in Psalms) as level `1`; `\s2` and the Psalm 119 acrostic letters `\qa` ("א", "ALEPH", two lines each) as level `2`; the 5 `\d` lines outside a verse ("Psalms 1–41" and the like, under each BOOK heading) as `d`.
- **Headings inside a verse:** 6 speaker headings in Song of Solomon fall inside a verse and sit between its segments: 1:4 "The Friends" and "The Bride", 5:1 "The Friends", 6:13 "The Bridegroom", 7:9 "The Bride", 8:5 "The Bride".
- **Segments:** `\q1` and `\q2` are kinds 1 and 2; `\qr` (223 right-aligned lines: "Selah", "declares the LORD", refrains) is 1; `\p`, `\m`, `\pmo` and `\pc` (11 centered inscriptions, given as prose) are prose that starts a paragraph; the list items `\li1` (1,343) and `\li2` (194) are `i`, starting a paragraph (the deeper indent of `\li2` is folded into `i`).
- **Breaks inside a verse:** 1,777 paragraph starts (`p`, 139 of them with a stanza break too) and 830 stanza breaks (`b`, those 139 included) fall inside a verse and are on its segments, such as Genesis 1:5's "And there was evening…" (`p0`) and Genesis 2:23's poem after "And the man said:" (`b1`). Every one of the source's 3,303 `\b` is either a verse's `b` flag (2,473) or a segment's (830).
- **Footnotes:** all 4,854. The note's words (`\ft`, `\fq`, `\fqa`, `\fv`), with references inside a note (`\ref`) kept as their visible text ("See Revelation 7:17."). `\fv` verse numbers inside a note are kept (35 notes, such as "BYZ and TR include 21 But this kind does not come out…"); in 44 places the USFM runs two parts of a note together ("Sheminith\ft is probably", "\fv 18\fv*...For") and a space was put between the words.
- **Book names:** each book's `\toc2`, except Psalms, whose `\toc2` is "Psalm" (the BSB's running head for one psalm): `books.tsv` says "Psalms".

Left out:

- The 1,325 parallel-passage lines (`\r`, such as "(John 1:1–5; Hebrews 11:1–3)"): cross-references.
- The 16 verses the BSB omits (see below), which have no text of their own.
- Book headers (`\id`, `\usfm`, `\h`, `\toc1` to `\toc3`, `\mt1`, `\mt2`); `books.tsv` keeps the short name.

The BSB has no deuterocanon; the archive holds just the 66 books.

Against the KJV's versification, the BSB lacks 16 verses: Matthew 17:21, 18:11, 23:14; Mark 7:16, 9:44, 9:46, 11:26, 15:28; Luke 17:36, 23:17; John 5:4; Acts 8:37, 15:34, 24:7, 28:29; Romans 16:24. The BSB leaves them out of its text and gives each in a footnote on the verse before ("BYZ and TR include 21 But this kind does not come out except by prayer and fasting"); `bsb.txt` has an empty line for each. It has no verse the KJV lacks and joins no verses.

## Rights

- **Public domain.** The publisher dedicated the BSB to the public domain as of April 30, 2023, linking the dedication to CC0 1.0, and says "All uses are freely permitted" and "Licensing is not required for any use." Attribution and links are "appreciated but not required". Nothing in the statements is weaker than public domain.
- **The name.** The publisher invites apps "that maintain the verbatim text" to use the Berean name, and asks that "derivative works that vary from the official text" not use it. The app's BSB verse text is the publisher's own `bsb.txt`, verbatim in every verse (checked by `check.py`), with the publisher's own headings, titles and footnotes; only cross-reference lines are left out. It may carry the name "Berean Standard Bible".
