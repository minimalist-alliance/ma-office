# ADR 0094: Office backgrounds

**Status:** Proposed. Built on `sub/zen-backgrounds`. Desk app only; no contract change.
**Builds on:** design 3.12 (themes and the contrast table), the rule "The layout follows the member" (`web/CLAUDE.md`), ADR 0064 (the active desk's ring on the ground), the Desk styles work (`prefs` and an unknown id kept as it came).
**Date:** 2026-11-10.

## Context
The Zen theme (the Dusk id with a new look, `sub/zen-theme`) wants a calm picture behind the office rather than a flat gradient: about ten relaxing nature scenes, photographs with an empty quiet middle, from the Product Designer's `design-drafts/zen-backgrounds`. The office's canvas is transparent over `.ma-shell::before`, the ground gradient on its own compositor layer, so a picture is a full-bleed layer there, under the desks. Two rules must hold: the contrast table (text and the active desk's ring on the ground), and "never sluggish" (a camera move re-rasterizes nothing that isn't the canvas). The initial JS is at its 250 KB budget.

## Decisions

### 1. The choice is the member's, in the layout document
`prefs.background` in the layout document: a scene's id (`^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,31}$`, as a desk style's) or `"none"`. Absent means the theme's default. Platform stores the document as it comes, so this is no contract change. It follows the member to every browser, like a desk's style. An id this build doesn't know (a newer build's, or a removed scene) stays stored, goes back as it came, and shows the theme's default. Choosing applies at once and saves like any other change; no Undo, the next pick is one.

### 2. The default is per theme
With nothing chosen, Zen (stored id `dusk`) shows the manifest's first scene; Light and Dark show none. A member may pick a scene in any theme for now (`BACKGROUNDS_IN` in `theme/backgrounds.ts` lists the themes where one can show; `DEFAULT_SCENE_IN` the ones with a default), so the Designer can narrow either with a one-line change. Theme stays per browser, so the scene shown is the pref resolved against this browser's theme.

### 3. The picture shows; contrast on the ground comes from halos, not from the wash
A first cut raised the scrim until the whole contrast table held over every scene's average colour (0.90 in Light, 0.94 in Dusk), which defeated the feature: `text.secondary` and `ring.activeDesk` are tuned to the edge on worldBase, so any scene darker or lighter than the ground needs a near-opaque wash. The Lead's decision (reversible UI): the scrim is light, text never sits on bare ground, and the rings carry their own contrast.
- **Text never sits on bare ground.** Every word over the office is on a surface of its own: the bottom bar's hints, the desk switch and the zoom control are `surface.chrome` pills (opaque in Dusk, 0.96 in Light and Dark), the office's notices are `surface.overlay`, and menus and windows are their own surfaces, all covered by the token tests. `backgrounds.shell.spec.ts` holds `.hints`, `.desk-nav` and `.zoom-ctl` at an alpha of 0.9 or more while a scene shows, so nobody later makes one transparent over a picture. The scrim therefore guards no text and isn't measured against any.
- **The scrim is a darken, never a fog.** The owner (Nov 10, 9:30 pm MT): "I don't like how reducing the contrast ruins the deep dark black blues. but the whole thing needs to be 'toned down'." So the picture itself is toned down in the art pipeline (grade v8), and in the app `surface.backdrop` meets the picture as `surface.backdropBlend` says (a `normal` | `multiply` token, `--ma-surface-backdrop-blend` on the scrim's pseudo-element): Zen `rgba(12, 16, 26, 0)` multiply, a no-op: its rings pass on the bare toned picture, and the owner approved the graded photo under the navy band only and called anything darker "depressing" (the alpha rises only if the v8 art's averages make the rings' rule need it); Dark `rgba(12, 16, 26, 0.30)` multiply, the lowest at which the rings' rule (below) holds on the v8 set's averages; Light `rgba(233, 235, 239, 0.40)` source-over, the lowest that passes (moonlit-sea, a night scene with no tone-down, sets it), since a light colour multiplies to nothing and a dark multiply would put a near-black ground under a light UI. The band is navy in every theme. The manifest's `grade.scrim` (`{ mode: "none" }` in v8) is the pipeline's note that the photo is pre-toned; the app doesn't read it, since Light and Dark need their scrim whatever the art's grade, and the themes' tokens decide.
- **The halo**, `ring.halo`, one token per theme, dark in every theme: Light `rgba(16, 20, 28, 0.95)`, Dusk `rgba(30, 27, 23, 0.95)`, Dark `rgba(5, 6, 8, 0.95)`. While `<html>` has `data-backdrop`, the active desk's ring on the canvas gets a 1 px line in it just outside (`panelSprite`, part of the sprite's key, latched so a scene arriving mid-zoom changes no key; redrawn when a scene comes or goes, at rest or at the camera's settle), and so does a focus ring over the ground: the proxies' `::after` ring by a 1 px `box-shadow`, the bars' and circles' controls by a shadow under the global outline that fills the 2 px gap and one pixel beyond (one shadow can't leave the gap clear), so there the ring sits between two dark lines. Menus and windows are surfaces of their own and get none.
- **The test** (`backgrounds.test.ts`), for every theme and scene, with the scene as its average colour under the scrim, composed per theme (multiply: `under * (1 - a + a * c)` per channel; normal: source-over): for `ring.activeDesk` and `focus.ring`, either the ring or the halo (over that ground) reaches 3:1 on the ground, and the ring always reaches 3:1 on its halo, so the pair reads as one ring. The halos' alpha is high because a dark halo over a mid-tone ground only reaches 3:1 when nearly opaque; Light's focus ring on its halo is the tightest pair at 3.0.
- **The set is the graded art** (grade v8 in the manifest's `grade`, the photos toned down in the pipeline, dark blues, averages `#0a1837` to `#4a6278`), ten scenes with ocean-cliff first and so Zen's default: ocean-cliff, mountain-lake, misty-mountains, snow-ridge, pebble-shore, glacier-lagoon, snowy-forest, fjord, moonlit-sea, peaceful-city (the owner approved `ocean-cliff` as the graded photo under the navy band only). The manifest's `grade.edge_band` carries the bands' definition. A regrade or a new scene must pass the test again, with the alphas raised in 0.05 steps where it doesn't.
- **Edge bands** (every theme, only while a scene shows): under the flat wash, on the same pseudo-element and layer, the top and bottom edges carry `surface.backdropBand` (`rgba(12, 16, 26, 0.40)`, navy) fading to transparent at 14 % of the height, and the sides none, so the bars' controls sit on a steadier ground; the art pipeline measures text contrast in those bands with exactly this definition, and a manifest's `grade.edge_band` (`{ alpha, top_bottom, sides, color }`, `sides` 0 for none) overrides it at runtime, so a future art set can tune it without a release.
- The picture is blurred 2 px in Light and Dark, so no detail competes with the desks; Zen shows it unblurred, as approved: nothing sits on the photo there but the band.

### 4. Loading never moves the fast start
Nothing of a scene is in the initial JS or CSS. `theme/boot.ts` paints the scene this browser last showed, in this theme, from `localStorage['ma.prefs.background']` (id, theme, average colour, placeholder) on the first frame, as it does the theme. After the canvas's first paint, `Shell.tsx` fetches `theme/backdrop.ts`, which reads `backgrounds/manifest.json` (served as-is from `public/`, about 4 KB) and adds a `<picture>`: AVIF `<source>` then WebP `<img>`, `srcset` of 960, 1920, 2560 and 3840 wide, `sizes="100vw"`, `decoding="async"`, `fetchpriority="low"`, `object-fit: cover`. It fades in over 300 ms when loaded, and not at all under `prefers-reduced-motion`. A browser saving data (`navigator.connection.saveData`, `prefers-reduced-data`) stops at 1920. The layer has `will-change: transform`, like the gradient, so a camera move costs nothing more. "Plain background" hides it with the gradient. The picker (`chrome/BackgroundPicker.tsx`) is its own chunk, fetched when the account menu opens.

### 5. Files and the manifest
`web/public/backgrounds/<id>/<width>.<format>` and `manifest.json` (`version: 1`, `scenes[]` with `id`, `name`, `widths`, `formats`, `average`, `luminance`, `placeholder`, `source`), the Designer's build output copied in whole. Served under the release's base (`/v/<version>/backgrounds/`), so a release carries its own scenes and a cached one never changes under a member. Adding a scene is adding its folder and manifest entry; the contrast test then judges it.

## Consequences
- Initial JS 241.7 to 242.2 KB gzipped; the layer and the picker are two lazy chunks.
- The picture shows through in Light and Dusk; less in Dark, where the ring sets the alpha. A lighter wash there means a brighter ring under a backdrop, the Designer's call.
- The halo is a departure from the flat look only while a scene shows; "Plain background" and a theme without a scene draw the rings as before.
- Publishing must serve `.avif` as `image/avif` and `.webp` as `image/webp`; the harness does.

## Addendum, 2026-11-11: Zen only
The owner: "Changing backgrounds should only be available in zen mode". So `BACKGROUNDS_IN` is `['dusk']` (Zen): Light and Dark draw no scene and the account menu's Background picker shows only in Zen. Light's wash (0.40) and Dark's multiply (0.30) are gone (their `surface.backdrop` is transparent and never drawn). The member's `prefs.background` is kept when they switch to Light or Dark and shows again in Zen; "Plain background" is unchanged. Under System, a dark system with "Use Zen when the system is dark" on resolves to Zen and so gets the scene.
