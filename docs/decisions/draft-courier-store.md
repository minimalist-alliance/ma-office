## Context
- Courier carries email (the desk's own address and the member's linked mailboxes), sealed messages between members' desks, and iMessage and texts through the member's own Mac, each person's media in one thread (design §8.1, ADR 0016). Everything is kept on the desk.
- Every transport has to hand messages to one place that stores each once, however often it comes: after a retry, a re-sync, a wake, a crash (design §8.4's idempotent sink).
- Untrusted bytes never parse inside deskd. A stranger's mail is hostile until shown otherwise: a malformed MIME tree, an HTML bomb, a References header aimed at a contact's thread, a display name that's an address.
- The agent read tools (agent tools §12) read this store, and need an id and an order that never change across a re-sync.
- deskd's footprint rules (services/CLAUDE.md): JNI on platform threads of its own, no periodic timers but the ones a contract needs, no lazy init, and nothing logged of a payload.

## Decisions

### 1. Where it lives
```
/data/courier/            0700, uid 1100 (deskd), its own mount in deskd's container
  courier.db              SQLite: WAL, synchronous=FULL, secure_delete, foreign keys, schema in user_version;
                          each raw message up to 64 KiB gzipped in blob_data, beside its row
  blobs/ab/cd/<sha256>.eml.gz   each larger raw message exactly as it came, gzip
  blobs/.staged/          a blob file written before its row commits (section 3)
  keys/                   the vault (section 9): master.key and <accountId>.sealed
/data/desk/courier-switch the kill switch (section 12)
```
- **One writer thread, two readers.** Every write runs on one platform thread; reads run on two read-only connections of their own, which see the last commit and never wait for a write, so a big sync doesn't hold up a member opening a thread. Page caches: 4 MiB for the writer, 2 MiB each reader.
- **`synchronous=FULL`:** a commit is on disk when it returns, because a transport acknowledges its source (an IMAP flag, the relay's ack) only after it.
- **A schema newer than the build is refused** at open, as desk-agent's stores do: a rollback onto migrated data fails closed (every call `unavailable`, `reason: store`), never half-reads.
- **deskd depends on `sqlite-jdbc` now,** as desk-agent does, and the image takes its native library out of the jar at build time (`/app/native`, `jvm.options`). The AppCDS training opens a store and calls `courier.*`, so an image whose library doesn't load fails its build.
- **Blobs are gzip, not the plan's zstd:** the JDK has it, and zstd would be a second JNI library in deskd. A new suffix is all a later change needs.
- **Most blobs are rows, not files.** A raw message whose gzip is at most 64 KiB goes in `blob_data` (keyed by its SHA-256), in its message's own transaction; a larger one is a file. Measured in a 100,000-message run with a file each: at 71,357 files, 103 MB of gzip took 466 MB of disk, as each file costs at least a 4 KiB block and an inode. Mail with an attachment is the file's case, where the block is noise.

### 2. The model
Design §8.2's, with what the plan adds. The tables: `accounts` (never a secret), `own_addresses` (the member's, for telling in from out), `contacts`, `endpoints` (`kind` email, ma, phone or apple; normalized `value`; `standing`), `blocks`, `channels` (`direct:<contact>`, `group:<hash of the members>`, `list:<List-Id>`, `self`), `channel_members`, `threads` (an email conversation inside a channel), `messages`, `message_refs`, `recipients`, `attachments`, `channel_lanes` (the per-lane summary rows a list reads), `cursors` (a transport's place), `drafts`, `outbound`, `agent_cursors`, `blob_data`, `blob_gc`, `totals` (what `courier.status` counts, kept by triggers in each write's transaction) and `messages_fts`.
- **A message's id is desk-made: a ULID,** monotonic within the process and carried on from the newest at open, so ids never collide and always sort in the order they were made.
- **`received_at` is the desk's, and never goes back:** each is at least the last one, and after a restart at least a millisecond past the newest stored. So `(received_at, id)` is a strict order matching the order of storage, whatever the clock does. A message that becomes visible to agents later (let in, out of Spam, read after the parser was away) gets a new `received_at` past every other (section 11).
- **`sort_at` is what lists and threads order by:** the message's own date, never later than when it arrived, so a sender can't pin mail to the top with a date in the future.
- **`sort_key` is the search's order and the FTS rowid:** `sort_at` in milliseconds times 2^20, plus the message's arrival among that millisecond's (a full millisecond takes the next one's room), so it's unique, newest is largest, and it never changes. The index hands a search its matches newest first and the query stops at a page (section 7).
- **`seq` (`INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT`) is the row's own key,** never reused, which the child tables hang from.

### 3. The idempotent sink
Every transport hands messages to `CourierStore.accept(batch, cursor)`.
- **The key is `(medium, externalId)`** (design §8.4), unique in the table. The same key twice is stored once, and the second answer is `DUPLICATE` with the first's id. A duplicate is found on a reader before any parse, so a re-sync costs no sandbox work.
- **An email's `externalId` doesn't depend on where the source keeps it:** `ExternalIds.email(account, Message-ID, raw)` is a hash of the account, the message's id and its exact bytes. A UIDVALIDITY reset or a move brings no second copy, and two different messages that share a Message-ID stay two.
- **The member's own sent mail coming back** (a Sent folder, a list's echo) with the Message-ID Courier gave it is the same message: `DUPLICATE`, and its raw bytes are kept if the first copy had none.
- **A batch and its cursor commit together, or neither does.** A full disk, a refused cursor or a crash leaves the transport where it was, and its next try brings the same messages, which the key folds.
- **Answers:** `STORED`, `DUPLICATE`, `PENDING` (kept with its raw bytes, waiting for the parser) or `REFUSED` with a reason (`account`, `medium`, `external_id`, `content`); a refusal keeps nothing.
- **Blobs are crash-safe.** A blob in `blob_data` commits with its row or not at all. A file is written to `.staged/`, synced and renamed before the row that names it commits; the writer's thread then moves it into place, or drops it if no row names it. A crash leaves at most a staged file, which the next open places or removes. A purge records the blob in `blob_gc` in the transaction that drops its last row, and its row or file goes after the commit, on the writer's thread, never while a write in flight may name it. The same bytes twice (two accounts, a re-sync) are one blob.
- **Limits:** raw over 48 MiB isn't kept (the source still has it); the message is stored as unreadable, `too_large`. Over 40 MB the sandbox refuses (section 4).

### 4. Parsing in `desk-parse` (amends ADR 0097, proposed; built Nov 14)
- **deskd never parses MIME or HTML.** `desk-parse`'s Courier mode (`services/courier-parse`, its README) reads it in ADR 0097's sandbox.
  - **Where it runs:** a second instance of the same sandbox, `desk-parse-courier`: the same image (desk-agent's), the same wall (uid 1113, no network, no `/data`, no capabilities, a read-only root, 160 MiB, 64 tasks) and the same `desk-svc -accept` dispatcher. Its own socket, `/run/desk-parse-courier/sock/parse.sock`, which serves uid 1100 (deskd) only, one message at a time.
  - **Why a second instance:** desk-agent's instance serves uid 1103 alone, one file at a time, so a big first sync would starve the Agent's attachments.
  - **The command:** `java @/app/courier-parse.options`, which has `parse.options`' JVM flags line for line (`-Xmx72m`, the security manager) and the main `CourierWorker --from-stream`.
  - **Not baked yet:** the image's half (the unit, `tmpfiles.d`, the socket, deskd's read-only mount, a slot's `svc-spec`, `verify.sh`) is the System Engineer's. The exact change is in `docs/sub-status/mm-courier-parse.md`.
- **The wire:** one ASCII line (`courier1 message`, `courier1 part <id>`, `courier1 image <id>|-`, `courier1 archive <id>`), then the bytes. The answer is `OK ...` and a body, or `ERR <reason>`.
  - **Part ids** are IMAP's section numbers, so the same bytes always give the same ids.
  - **The worker** reads all it's sent before answering, and answers `ERR timeout` at 10 s from its start.
  - **deskd** (`CourierSandbox`) writes on a thread of its own while it reads the answer, holds one request at a time, retries `err busy` for 15 s, and closes the connection at 20 s whatever happens. That's past `desk-svc`'s own 15 s kill and its clean-up, so a stream always ends by `desk-svc`'s close, and a worker that stays silent can't be there for the next request (Security CP-L1). Answers are read by a JSON reader of its own, capped at 400,000 tokens and nesting 16, so a hostile answer inside the size cap can't fill deskd's heap (CP-L4).
- **The limits:** 2,000 MIME entities (the message and each multipart counted), nesting 20, 40 MiB a message, 10 s a parse. Over any of them the answer is a refusal, never a half-parsed message.
- **The answer is `parse-courier 1`,** a JSON object that `ParsedMessage.fromWire` checks field by field and caps: `{v: 1, from: {name, address}, replyTo, to, cc, bcc: [{name, address}], subject, date (ms), messageId, inReplyTo, references (the headers' text), listId, listUnsubscribe (bool), precedence, autoSubmitted, text, textTruncated, html (sanitized), attachments: [{part, name, type, bytes, contentId, inline, danger}]}`.
  - **Cleaning:** strings lose C0 and C1 controls, every format character (bidi controls, zero-width characters, the tag characters), line and paragraph separators, the Hangul fillers and lone surrogates. Text keeps only the zero-width joiner and non-joiner, which scripts and emoji need. Addresses are normalized, and one holding any of those or any kind of space is dropped.
  - **On every path:** `ParsedMessage`'s constructors do this, so a structured transport's message (`ma`, iMessage) and a parser's result are cleaned and capped as the sandbox's answer is (Security CS-L2).
  - **Caps:** ids are parsed from the headers' own text; lists stop at 500 addresses and 2,000 parts, and a part id twice is one part; text stops at 2 MiB (`textTruncated`), and HTML over 4 MiB is dropped. Anything that isn't that shape is a refusal.
  - **The sandbox cleans too:** it cleans the same way before it answers, so what an agent's model reads and what the member sees agree.
- **HTML is sanitized twice, inside the sandbox** (the plan §3.1's first wall). The office's scriptless frame with its own CSP is the second, not built (Wave 1.6).
  - **Pass 1** builds a new tree from an allow-list: text, tables, basic blocks and phrases, and inline styles cut to basic properties with no function but the colour ones, no `@`, no escape and no position tricks. Scripts, styles, frames, objects, form controls, SVG, MathML, templates, `meta`, `link` and `base` go with their content. Anything else is unwrapped.
  - **Pass 2** reads pass 1's output again with an HTML5 parser, as a browser would. It must serialize to the same string (no mutation XSS), and every element, attribute and value must be one pass 1 writes, each checked again. If it isn't, the HTML is dropped and the text kept.
- **Nothing in the HTML can load anything, so a pixel or a link never fetches a byte.**
  - **A link** has no `href`: `data-ma-href` holds its destination made plain (ASCII host, so a lookalike shows as punycode; user-info gone; known tracking parameters gone), `data-ma-host` its host, `data-ma-warn` what to warn of (`mismatch` when its words name another domain, `ip`, `punycode`, `userinfo`, `tracker`), and `title` the full destination, which a pointer shows with no script (§3.2).
  - **A remote image** has no `src`: `data-ma-src` holds its address without tracking parameters, for the member's "Show images", which the desk fetches (not built) and this mode draws again (`courier1 image -`).
  - **A tracking pixel** is removed outright (§3.4): its host is on ADR 0076's pinned lists (EasyPrivacy, as a host list the image's bake writes and the sandbox mounts read-only), it's 2 px or smaller, it's hidden, or its path is an open-tracking one.
  - `cid:` images are `data-ma-cid`. A `data:` image is decoded and drawn again, or dropped.
  - **The text,** for a message with no plain part, is rendered from pass 1's tree without what a style hides, each link as its words then `<destination>`.
- **Images are drawn again from their pixels** (§2.7, §3.4): PNG, JPEG, GIF and BMP. The size is checked before decoding (30,000 px a side, 50 megapixels, 12 for a progressive JPEG). They're decoded subsampled to 2,048 px, and come out as PNG with transparency or JPEG without. No metadata survives; anything else is `unsupported`.
- **`danger`** comes from the real extension (hidden characters and Windows' trailing dots removed) and the bytes, never the claimed type alone. The words, most dangerous first:

  | Word | What it is |
  |---|---|
  | `program` | executables, installers, Java and Android packages |
  | `script` | anything an interpreter runs |
  | `macros` | an Office file with macros: `docm` and the like, `vbaProject.bin` in a ZIP, `_VBA_PROJECT` in OLE2 |
  | `shortcut` | a shortcut or link file |
  | `disk_image` | ISO, DMG, VHD and the like |
  | `archive_programs` | a ZIP or tar whose list holds any of the above |
  | `html` | a web page or SVG |
  | `archive_unread` | RAR, 7z, cab, a compressed tar, or a ZIP too large to list |
- **An archive's file list** is read without unpacking (§2.8): a ZIP's central directory from its last MiB, ZIP64 included, and a tar's headers as they stream past.
- **While the sandbox can't be reached** an email is stored `pending` with its raw bytes, out of every list, and read by the hourly sweep once it answers.
  - **"Can't be reached"** means: no socket (an image before the Courier sandbox), a refused connection, `desk-svc`'s own `err` lines, or a worker that doesn't know the request.
  - **Refused:** a message the sandbox refuses is stored with `parse: {state: refused, reason}` and its raw bytes: "This message couldn't be read safely", with the raw download (not built). An answer that ends before its line twice is `too_complex` if a small message of deskd's own then reads, and `pending` if that ends empty too (a sandbox that can't run any worker, such as a desk-agent release from before this mode, Security CP-I5). No answer by the deadline is `timeout`, and an answer that isn't what was asked for is `answer`.
  - **More than one From** (two fields, or two mailboxes in one) is refused `malformed` (RFC 5322 allows one; RFC 7489 §6.6.1 allows refusing), so "confirmed" and "own mail" never pair a DMARC verdict with an address it didn't check (Security CP-L2). **For Wave 2:** each transport hands over the domain its DMARC result is for (`header.from` from the provider's own Authentication-Results, or SES's verdict's domain), and `Ingest` counts `dmarcPassed()` only when that domain equals the parsed From's (lowercase, IDNA). That also covers a compromised worker reporting another From.
- **Proven** (the status doc has the counts):
  - **The corpus:** the test kit's 5,155 messages, every one read or cleanly refused, in-process and through the socket into the store.
  - **Hostile HTML:** 351 vectors, none keeping anything that runs or loads.
  - **The fuzzer:** 10 million mutated inputs, with no crash and no escape.
  - **A real browser:** Chromium, scripts on and no CSP, showing every sanitized document with no script run and no request.
  - **The real sandbox on Docker:** every limit held under 160 MiB and in time.

### 5. Routing: one channel per person
Design §8.4's order, made safe:
1. **A thread match,** by JWZ's rules in any order of arrival: a thread that holds one of the message's parents (References and In-Reply-To, at most 100 kept: the root and the last 99), a message naming one of the same parents (a sibling whose parent never came), a message naming this one (a child that came first), or a message with this one's id (the same mail through another account). Threads these join merge, within one channel.
2. **A list's channel,** by List-Id.
3. **One sender's own channel,** `direct:<contact>`: Dana's email, her texts and her sealed messages are one thread.
4. **A group's channel,** keyed by the set of people other than the member.
5. **The member's own mail** goes to the channel of its recipients, or `self` with none.

**The member's own mail** is what the transport says is theirs (a Sent folder, Courier's own echo of its send), or an email from one of the member's addresses whose DMARC passed (section 6). **A From header alone never is** (Security CS-M1, Nov 13): anyone can write the member's address there. Mail that only claims it is a stranger's, from that address: it joins no thread by id, isn't People or read, lets no one in, and attaches no bytes to the member's sent message.

**A thread match never lets a stranger in.** A Message-ID isn't secret: it travels in every reply, list archive and forward. So a thread takes a message only if it's the member's own, its sender is a member of the thread's channel, someone a message in that thread was addressed to (a contact's reply-all that brought them in), or, for a list's thread, a poster to that list. Otherwise it routes by its sender (section 6), and a References header aimed at a contact's thread lands the stranger in Requests.

**The subject fallback is for replies only:** a message with no ids whose subject says it's a reply (`Re:`, `AW:`, `SV:`, `回复：` and others, after any `[list]` tag) joins the newest thread in its own channel with the same subject within 30 days. The same words without a reply prefix start a new thread.

Contacts are scoped to the desk. An `ma` endpoint and an `email` one with the same address are one contact (one address does both, [draft-courier-address](draft-courier-address.md)). The member can merge two contacts (`courier.contact.merge`): their channels become one.

### 6. Lanes and standing
A lane is kept per message, so one person's personal mail and their receipts can be in People and Updates at once; a lane's list groups by channel. An endpoint's standing is `known` (let in, a contact, or someone the member wrote to), `unknown` or `blocked`.
- **Arriving mail, in order:** a blocked sender (by address, or by domain and its subdomains) is **Spam**; the member's own mail is **People**; a spam or virus verdict from the transport is **Spam**; a known sender is **People**, or **Updates** for bulk mail (`List-Unsubscribe`, or `Precedence: bulk`, `list` or `junk`); a stranger replying in a thread the member started is **People**; a stranger the learning filter judges spam is **Spam** (section 17); any other stranger is **Other** through a linked mailbox (years of a member's own mail mustn't land in Requests) and a **Request** through this desk's address, the member address or iMessage.
- **Writing to someone lets them in,** a linked mailbox's Sent folder included: their Requests and Other move to People or Updates.
- **A Request's card:** "Let in" (known; everything they sent moves), "Not now" (the card goes until they write again; the mail stays, and goes after 90 days) and "Block" (every endpoint of theirs, and their mail outside Trash, to Spam).
- **Moving a stranger's mail to People or Updates lets them in;** moving it from Spam is "Not spam". A blocked sender stays blocked until Unblock.
- **Done** takes a row out of its lane's list until a newer message comes.
- **The sender line comes from authentication** (plan §3.5): a message carries its transport's SPF, DKIM and DMARC results, and `senderConfirmed` is true only when DMARC passed. The results come only from the transport's own verifier (SES's verdict, or a linked mailbox's topmost `Authentication-Results` field alone, when it carries the provider's authserv-id, read by the sandbox and chosen by deskd; nothing below it is read or merged in, Security's CI-M2), never a header the sender wrote, and a missing result is unconfirmed. **A DMARC pass counts only for the domain it was checked for, which must be the From's own** (Security CP-L2's second half, built in Wave 2.1): each transport hands over that domain (`dmarcDomain`), and a pass for another domain, or with none, is kept as `unaligned`. For `ma`, the proof is the envelope's signature, not DMARC. A display name that holds an address is dropped; a name is a hint on the endpoint, never identity, and the member's own name for a contact is never replaced by one.

### 7. Search
SQLite FTS5 over the subject, the sender's name and address, the recipients, the text and the attachment names: `unicode61 remove_diacritics 2`, prefix indexes of 2 and 3, external content on `messages`, kept by triggers that fire only when an indexed column changes. Notebook search's rules: every word must match, by prefix, ignoring case and accents; "quoted phrases"; and `from:`, `to:`, `has:attachment`, `before:`, `after:` (the member's days, in their zone), `in:<lane>` and `is:unread`. **Nothing typed is an FTS5 operator:** every word and phrase is quoted, its quotes doubled. Newest first; snippets of 200 characters with where the words are. Spam and Trash only when asked (`in:spam`). Measured at 100,000 messages in `docs/sub-status/lsub-courier-store.md`.
- **No search sorts.** The index's rowid is `sort_key` (section 2), so FTS5 gives the matches newest first and the query stops at a page, with a snippet made only for the rows returned; before a fix, a common word's search computed a snippet for every match and sorted them all, 77 ms at 3,000 messages. Each match's lane, channel and flags are read from one index (`messages_key`), so a match that doesn't pass costs a probe, not a row. Dates (`before:`, `after:`, the agent tools' days) are rowid bounds the index seeks to. Without words, `messages_key`, a lane's index or the channel's does the same. `SearchPlanTest` holds every search's plan to no sort. A page's cursor is the last hit's `sort_key`, so deleting a hit between pages skips nothing.
- **Known limit:** `unicode61` treats a run of CJK characters as one word, so a search finds it from the run's start only. FTS5's trigram tokenizer would fix it at about three times the index's size; left for a measurement.
- **FTS5's `secure-delete` is on:** a delete rewrites the index's pages at once, so a message deleted for good leaves no word of it in the index (tested by planting a canary and finding it in no file of the store).

### 8. The outbound queue
- **The member's send** turns a draft into a message in its channel at once (`queued`, as the member's own) and a queue row `held` for **10 seconds of Undo**. Then it goes to its transport.
- **A failure that can pass retries** with backoff (1, 2, 5, 10, 30 minutes, 1, 2, then every 4 hours, each with up to a tenth more at random) until it's sent or 4 days have gone, and then it's `failed`. A refusal fails at once. A queued, retrying or failed send can be taken back to a draft; one going or gone can't (`too_late`).
- **A desk that stops mid-send** takes the queue up at the next open: a row left `sending` goes back in the queue marked `interrupted`, and its transport is told (`maybeSentBefore`), so it can look in the Sent folder before sending again. An SMTP submission can't be taken back, so this is the most that's honest: at most one doubled send in that window, and none lost. **A `Retry` after a result that leaves it unclear whether the message went** (the connection lost after DATA's final dot) is `Retry(error, unclear: true)`, which marks the row `interrupted` too (Security, Nov 13; built in Wave 2.1), so the next try is told `maybeSentBefore`, and a linked mailbox's then looks for the Message-ID in Sent first. The RFC Message-ID stays the same across tries, so receivers can fold a double.
- **A send needs a transport for its medium and account** (`Outbox.Sender`), or `courier.send` answers `unavailable`, `reason: medium`, before anything is done: until a provider is live, desk addresses receive only (the plan, §4.2).
- **Courier never sends by itself.** A row exists only for the member's own `courier.send`: no auto-reply, no vacation reply, no read receipt, no agent's message. A blocked address is refused.
- **A reply keeps its place in the email thread:** `In-Reply-To` and `References` from the message it answers, and its thread, unless it's a private reply from a group's thread, which starts its own in the person's channel.

### 9. Linked accounts' sign-ins (built Nov 14; Wave 1.3)
ADR 0042's design in its own place, as the plan's §3.6 has it (`courier/vault/`).
- **The files:** `/data/courier/keys/` (0700), `master.key` (32 random bytes, 0600, made once), and one `<accountId>.sealed` (0600) per account.
  - **The format:** `MAKV`, a version byte, a random 12-byte nonce, then AES-256-GCM ciphertext and tag. The AAD is the magic and version, the account id and its kind (`imap`, `jmap`), each after its `u16be` length.
  - **Writes:** a temporary file, fsync, an atomic rename, then the directory fsynced.
- **What's sealed** (`SignIn`): the kind (`password`, `app_password`, `oauth`), the user name, the secret (the password, or the access token), the refresh token and its expiry, and the transport's connection settings. It's held as bytes and zeroed when closed; its `toString` names only its kind.
- **The `accounts` row is the commit point** (`LinkedAccounts`).
  - **Add:** the transport checks the sign-in with the provider (a `Verifier` it registers; Wave 2.1's), the sign-in is sealed, then the row is written. If sealing fails nothing is kept; if the row fails the sealed file goes.
  - **Change** (an OAuth refresh): sealed over the old one, atomically.
  - **Remove:** the row (with its mail unless kept), then the unlink.
  - **Start:** a sealed file no row names is deleted. A row whose file is missing or won't open reads as signed out (`signedIn: false`).
- **Fails closed:** a sealed file changed by any byte, cut, grown, moved to another account or kind, or sealed under another master key doesn't open, and says only which kind of failure it was. A bad master key is renamed aside, never overwritten.
- **Write-only from the office:** `courier.accounts.add` (API.md §21) takes the sign-in once. It's `unavailable` `{reason: medium}` until a transport for the kind registers its verifier, so nothing is kept unchecked. Only "signed in" ever goes back (`courier.accounts.list`'s `signedIn`).
- **The secret is in no row, no answer and no log, and never goes to the platform:** it reaches only the provider, through the desk's own transport. The audit canary (`VaultCanaryTest`) sends known secrets down every path and finds none in any log line at TRACE, on the console, in any RPC answer or in any file of the store.
- **Out of the download** (Security CP-L3): `tools/desk-data` leaves `courier/keys` out of the `courier` part, so a download never holds `master.key` beside the sealed sign-ins. A move reads through the same helper, so today it doesn't carry them either, and the member signs in again on the new desk. The plan's tick ("Include linked mailbox sign-ins", whose words must say the file then opens the mailboxes) and a move's carrying them are Wave 3.3's: a move may carry `keys/` only under the move's own protection, and if the platform could read a move, the member signs in again after it instead.
- **The honest limit** (ADR 0042, ADR 0074): `master.key` sits beside what it seals. Root on the running desk, or whoever holds the volume or a snapshot, can open it. Revoking at the provider is the only real revocation.

### 10. Attachments (built Nov 14; Wave 3.1; amends ADR 0048, proposed)
Named, typed and sized from the sandbox; never opened, run or previewed on the desk without a press; the sandbox's `danger` names programs, scripts, shortcuts, disk images, HTML and macro documents (`courier/CourierAttachments`; API.md §21, "Attachments").
- **Every byte comes from the sandbox:** `courier1 part` cuts a part out of the kept raw message, `image` draws an image again from its pixels, `archive` reads an archive's list without unpacking it. deskd copies what it's given and looks at the first bytes for a program's mark (Windows, ELF, Mach-O, `#!`, a shortcut), which makes a file `program` whatever its name or the sandbox said. Nothing else on the desk reads an attachment.
- **Dangerous types** are saved or downloaded only with `allowDanger: true`, the office's second press after it has said what the file is, and never previewed. A preview is only for PNG, JPEG, GIF and BMP.
- **"Save to Files"** copies through deskd's Files door (`AgentFiles`: the tree only deskd writes, its path rules, the quota and the floor, `files.changes`) into `Files/Courier/<date> <subject>/`, the date in the member's zone, and never replaces a file: the same bytes are `already`, other bytes under the name get `name (2).ext`. Names lose hidden, reordering and look-alike characters, slashes and what other systems refuse, leading dots and trailing dots and spaces, and keep their real extension when cut to 200 bytes. **The `untrusted` mark is Files' own:** an agent's `files_read` adds `U` for every file (agent tools §6), so a saved attachment carries it wherever it's moved, and nothing new was needed. An image saved this way isn't sent to the image check, as a Browser download isn't (ADR 0048 §8).
- **"Download", a preview and the raw message** go on `bulk` as `files.read` sends a file, so nothing of MA's sees the bytes. The bytes are staged in a file under `/data/courier/parts` that's unlinked as soon as it's written, so a slow download holds nothing in deskd's heap and nothing stays on the volume after it (or after deskd stops, however it stops); a leftover from a crash between creating and unlinking is removed at start. The bandwidth pause refuses a new download as it does `files.read`.
- **One extraction at a time,** the turn held for the bytes' whole life on the desk (Security CR3-M1), so attachments add at most a raw message (48 MiB, about twice that while it's unzipped) and one part (25 MiB) to deskd's heap; `CourierSandbox` reads a part's answer into exactly the size its line names, never twice over. A download is staged only if the volume keeps ADR 0035's 2 GiB floor after it (CR3-L2), and its transfer place is taken first. A dangerous type's Download says `application/octet-stream`, never the sender's claim, and the raw message of one with a dangerous attachment needs the second press too (CR3-I3).
- **Not built:** "Show images" for remote images (the sandbox's `image -` is there; the fetch, with the parse read's conditions, isn't).

### 11. What agents read (agent tools §12; the plan's §6 amendments, accepted by Security's stand-in read, Nov 13)
`CourierRead` is the seam the tools builder writes `courier_list`, `courier_search`, `courier_read` and `courier_new` on, with §12's names and fields, implemented on the real store by `AgentView`. The three amendments to §12:
1. The store is `courier.db`, not `chat.db`.
2. **Agents see People, Updates (marked `bulk`) and Other (marked `senderKnown: false`), never Requests, Spam or Trash.** Without Other, an airline's booking in a linked mailbox is invisible to the to-do agent. The lanes are in the SQL of every read, so no path returns another. A blocked sender's mail is in Spam, and reaches an agent only if the member moves it out themselves (Security CS-I1). A setting (`DESKD_COURIER_AGENTS_SEE_OTHER`, default `true`, kept by Security's read) turns Other off. A to-do made from an Other message names its sender as unconfirmed in its source label.
3. Each result carries `lane` and `accountLabel` ("Gmail", "This desk's address"), never the account's address.

**A linked mailbox's first sync of its old mail is never "new"** (Wave 2.1): those messages are `origin: backfill`, and `courier_new` reads by an index that leaves them out (`messages_received_new`), so linking a mailbox doesn't hand an agent years of mail as if it had just come. Mail of the last day before the link is `catchup`, which it does read; so is mail caught up after a desk slept.

**The cursor** is `(receivedAt, messageId)` (section 2). A message an agent couldn't see that becomes visible (let in, out of Spam or Trash, read after the parser was away) gets a new `receivedAt` past every other, so it's after any cursor. One stored cursor per agent; one behind the stored one is ignored. Tested across let-ins, a parser's backlog, the clock going both ways and restarts: none skipped, none twice.

**Text** is plain, each link as `[link: example.com]` (its domain only, in ASCII, so a lookalike shows as punycode), quoted mail folded to `Earlier messages: n`, 20,000 characters a page. `from` is the contact record of the message's sender endpoint, and **every result carries `senderConfirmed`** (Security CS-L3): true for the member's own and for an email whose DMARC passed, false for any other email (a missing result included), null for media with no DMARC. The tools show it beside `from`, so a forged From from a known contact reads as unconfirmed to the model, as it does to the member.

### 12. On, with a kill switch
Prelaunch features ship on (the owner, Nov 12). `/data/desk/courier-switch` turns things off: `off` (every `courier.*` but `courier.status` answers `unavailable`, `reason: switched_off`; the queue sends nothing; the agent tools aren't offered), `send-off` (the queue sends nothing), and `<medium>-off` for each channel's transport. A file that isn't a plain small file counts as `off`.
- **Who:** the owner's sessions on an adult's personal desk. A guild desk has no Courier (`forbidden`, `reason: scope`; guild chat is Commons', ADR 0080), a teen's desk none either (ADR 0098 decision 7: `forbidden`, `reason: teen`), and while the band isn't known calls get `unavailable`, `reason: age_band`.
- **No store** (no `/data/courier` on the image, a newer schema, a library that didn't load): every call `unavailable`, `reason: store`, and nothing else in deskd changes.

### 13. Retention (plan §2.9)
Hourly and at start: Trash and Spam go 30 days after they got there, Requests nobody answered after 90, finished sends' queue rows after 7 days. Deleting for good removes the row, its index entries (FTS5 `secure-delete`), its blob (row or file) once nothing names it, and then **every path that deletes for good** (Delete forever, Empty Trash, removing an account with its mail, the sweep) empties the WAL with a checkpoint; one a reader held back is owed and retried at the next sweep (Security CS-L1). The canary test scans the store's files while it's open, on each path, with a blob as a row and as a file. **The honest limit** (ADR 0074): a blob file can only be unlinked; on ext4 over EBS an overwrite doesn't reliably reach the same blocks, so it would add false assurance. Whoever holds the volume or a snapshot may still find blob files' blocks, WAL remnants and free pages; the privacy words say so. A key per blob, destroyed on delete, is the later fix. What a delete for good leaves on purpose: the sender's endpoint and contact (address and name), which `courier.contacts.list` still shows (Security CS-I3, a follow-up). Removing a linked account deletes its cursors, and its mail unless the member keeps it. Not built: the export's `courier` part (Wave 3.3), and removing at the provider too.

### 15. Linked mailboxes over IMAP and SMTP (built Nov 14; Wave 2.1)
`courier/linked/` in deskd, with its state in `courier.db` (schema 2), so a message and where it sits at the provider commit together. API.md §21, "Linked mailboxes", is what the office sees.
- **Its own IMAP client** (`ImapReader`, `ImapClient`): IDLE, CONDSTORE and QRESYNC (ENABLEd), UIDPLUS, MOVE, SPECIAL-USE, ESEARCH, LITERAL+, SASL-IR, Gmail's X-GM-EXT-1, each used only when the server has it, with fallbacks (COPY, \Deleted and UID EXPUNGE for MOVE; flags compared every 30 minutes and expunges by the UID list without CONDSTORE and QRESYNC). Rather than a mail library: it never builds a message object or parses MIME, so a message is a literal's bytes handed whole to the sink, and every size the server could send is bounded before it's allocated (a line, a literal, a list's depth, a command's whole answer).
- **The state** (`LinkedState`): `linked_folders` (each folder's UIDVALIDITY, how far the live path and the first sync have gone, its MODSEQ), `linked_locations` (a message's places: folder, UID, `\Seen`, the provider's labels, and its Message-ID, size, date and Gmail id), `linked_orphans` (a message whose last place went, until it's found again or a whole pass hasn't found it), `linked_ops` (the member's changes owed to the provider). Places commit in the sink's own transaction (`accept`'s sidecar).
- **0 missed, 0 doubled:** a UID the desk has a place for is never fetched again; a message moved or renumbered is matched by its Gmail id, or by its Message-ID with its size and date, without a fetch; whatever that misses, the sink's own key stores once. A folder's places under an old UIDVALIDITY are void, and no op acts on a folder whose numbering changed until the sync has started it over. Proven by a seeded soak of faults (`LinkedSoakTest`) against the kit's fake provider and GreenMail (the status doc).
- **Both ways:** the member's read, Trash, Spam, restore, delete for good and block are written as ops in `Actions`' own transactions; the transport applies them as STORE and MOVE (COPYUID records the new place) and never writes an op itself; a provider change to a message with an op owed waits, so the member's last word wins. The provider's flags, labels and moves (into Trash or Junk and back) come back as the desk's read state, labels and lanes, with no op. A message gone from every place for a whole pass goes to Trash on the desk; one gone from the provider's Trash is deleted for good.
- **Lanes:** Junk is a spam verdict, Trash goes to Trash, Sent (Gmail's `\Sent` label) is the member's own; strangers are Other.
- **Pacing:** newest first, Sent first, the last 12 months of every folder before older mail, at most 2 MiB/s, under the provider's daily download (Gmail: 2 GB of its 2.5), a shared 16 MiB of fetched-but-unstored bytes across accounts, and a provider's throttling backed off up to an hour. The first sync's old mail is `backfill` (section 11).
- **Where it connects** (Security's condition on the vault's question 4): the member's host names, resolved at every connect and refused if any address is loopback, link-local (IMDS included), private, shared, multicast, reserved or the desk's own, then reached by the checked address; only 993, 465 and 587; TLS 1.2 or 1.3 with the host checked, STARTTLS required on 587, never plain text.
- **Sign-ins:** app passwords and passwords, through `courier.accounts.add` and `courier.accounts.signin` only (the owner's session), checked against IMAP and SMTP, sealed (section 9), opened per connection and zeroed after. A refusal puts the account in `needs_you` and it waits for the member, looking again every 6 hours. OAuth is Wave 2.2's.
- **Sending:** at launch, linked mailboxes send (the plan §1.2): SMTP submission from the account's own address, a Sent copy where the provider files none, an unclear end looked for in Sent before a retry. On by default, with `imap-send-off` and `send-off` as kill switches.
- **The sender line:** a provider's results come from the topmost `Authentication-Results` field alone, when it carries its authserv-id (no field below it is read or merged in, CI-M2; Gmail's `mx.google.com` is the only one set until the real-provider runs record the others), and a DMARC pass counts only for the From's own domain (section 6).

### 17. Spam and safety (built Nov 14; Wave 3.2)
Requests stays the main filter (section 6). Around it (`courier/store/SpamFilter`, `Senders`; API.md §21, "Spam and safety"; schema 3):
- **The learning filter** (plan §2.8) is naive Bayes over a message's words (subject, sender, name, text, link and address domains, attachments' extensions, types and danger, bulk, the DMARC result), per desk, in `courier.db`. **Taught only by the member:** into Spam (Spam, Report, a Request's Block, a block) is spam; out of Spam (not to Trash), a Let in, and writing to someone (a few messages each) are wanted. `filter_trained` keeps what each message taught, so a change of mind moves its counts and nothing counts twice; at most 100 messages per action. **It judges only a stranger's mail that would be a Request or Other** (`Lanes.Facts.filterMayJudge`): never the member's own, a known sender's, a reply in the member's thread, or what a block or a verdict decided. The member's word outranks a guess. It acts after 10 of each, at a score of 0.95 (Robinson's token probabilities, s = 0.45, the 150 strongest clues combined by Fisher's chi-square, as SpamBayes does). What it screens is `screen: filter` with `lane_before` where it would have been, so Not spam returns it to Requests or Other and a later Let in brings it back; the member's own Spam on it clears the mark.
  - **Nothing leaves the desk.** No shared model or list; a token is kept only as SipHash-2-4 under the desk's random salt (`filter_state.salt`), so no word of anyone's mail is in the filter's tables as text (a canary test reads every file of the store). **The honest limit** (Security CR3-I1): the salt is in the same file, so whoever holds `courier.db` (the volume, a snapshot, a move) can test guessed words, addresses and domains against the counts; it's obfuscation against that reader, not secrecy, as the rest of `courier.db` is plain to them anyway. A key outside the database (the vault's) is the later fix. **On a linked mailbox the filter's verdict stays on the desk:** the provider isn't told, so the provider doesn't learn from the desk's guess; only the member's own Spam moves mail to its Junk. The honest consequence: what the filter screens there is in the provider's inbox still, and when the desk's Spam keeps it 30 days and lets it go, the provider still has it.
  - **Kill switch** `filter-off` in `courier-switch`: it judges nothing, and goes on learning.
- **Sender warnings** (plan §3.5), made once as a stranger's message arrives and kept with it (`messages.warn`): `not_usual_address` (the display name is a known contact's, by a folded, sorted key, `contacts.name_key`; a mailbox's role is never a name), `lookalike` (the registrable domain's UTS #39 skeleton equals one of the member's own domains, a known contact's by `endpoints.skel`, or a well-known one, MA's included; or it's one letter off a well-known name of six or more; or a well-known domain is written in front of another), `punycode` (an internationalized label that isn't UTS #39 "highly restrictive", or is spelled entirely in letters that pass for Latin: show the `xn--` form), `name_held_address`. A known sender has none: they were let in.
- **Report spam** (`courier.report`, plan §2.8): to Spam, a linked mailbox's to its provider's Junk, the filter taught, optionally each sender blocked, and for mail to this desk's own address a report of its sender owed to the mail door (`reports`; `CourierStore.owedReports` is Wave 2.5's seam to the platform's suppression list). **Only a sender the door's DMARC confirmed is reported,** never the member's own address (Security CR3-L1): a forged From must not put a third party on the suppression list. Platform should still scope a suppression to the reporting desk, or check it, before it goes wider. **Block skips a known contact's address on mail DMARC didn't confirm** (CR3-I2): that mail only claimed to be them.
- **Blocks end exactly** (Security CS-I2): a Request's Block writes an `address` block for each of the sender's endpoints; Unblock changes only the senders its row covered and no other row covers, and does nothing when there's no such row.
- **Linked mailboxes** (plan §2.4): the provider's Junk is a spam verdict whoever sent it; the Sent folder synced first makes its correspondents known, and older Sent mail synced later lets its correspondents' Other mail into People; other strangers are Other, never Requests.

## Consequences
- One idempotent sink and one cursor commit mean a transport can be simple: fetch, hand over, ack after the commit. Re-syncs are cheap.
- Threading in any order, with the admission rule, keeps a person's channel theirs: a stranger can't join it by knowing an id. A real reply from someone a contact brought in still threads.
- The raw bytes are kept, so a better parser later re-reads everything, and the export is exact. They cost the drive, compressed (the ratio measured is in the status doc).
- deskd carries SQLite's native library (one shared object for the image's architecture) and three connections' page caches (8 MiB at most) outside its heap.
- A crash between an SMTP submission and its answer can send a message twice; nothing can be lost.
- **Rejected:** Courier as its own service (a second JVM, 105 to 123 MiB, the task 08 spike); parsing MIME in deskd; one lane per channel (a person's newsletters would drag their channel out of People); threading by subject alone, or by References alone with no admission rule.
