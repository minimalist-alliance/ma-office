# 0075. Video islands: the one encoder crops to the player, and input follows the crop

**Status:** Proposed (task 47, the simplified browsing view). `desk-stream` 0.3.2 and `desk-rtc` 0.3.7 (0.3.6 before Security's T47S-1), over `desk-stream` 0.3.1 (ADR 0073): measured on one `c7g.xlarge` over 0.3.0; on `main`, pinned for the next image, and passed on the staging test desk with no island set (2026-11-07). **On by default, with a kill switch only (the owner's standing rule, 2026-11-07: a finished feature ships on, and its switch is never an opt-in), once the Security Analyst clears T47S-1, fixed in `desk-rtc` 0.3.7, and `deskd`'s half of T47-4 is built.** Until then `deskd` sends no `stream.crop` on a member's desk.
**Date:** 2026-11-07
**Amends:** [0030](0030-browser-modes-local-minified-60fps-normal.md) (islands, which it left at "a cropped region of the warm encoder"), [0034](0034-desk-media-path.md) (a new line on `stream.sock`), `desk-runtime.md` §4.3, §8.2 and §8.6 (byte 3 of an input event). Interfaces: `desk-runtime.md` §8.8.

## Context
- The owner, on the simplified view: "id still like to see videos in minified mode", and it has to be "elegant and something that people would actually find helpful and useful, not an inconvenience". Task 47: "Video shows as a live island inside the clean page: a cropped stream of just the player, smooth, with sound, streaming only while it's on screen."
- Design §7.4 already chose the transport: a crop of the warm encoder's output on the existing video track, with no new track, channel or signaling, and the encoder running only while an island is in view. Nothing built it, and nothing said how input reaches a cropped picture.
- Task 46 needs the CPU: in the simplified view the stream is the exception, so an island's cost is what a member watching a video costs the host.
- Security's T47-4 (`~/ma-coord/security-analyst.md`): a crop streams screen pixels, so whatever is drawn there gets the member's clicks; the crop must be clamped, input must carry which crop it was meant for, stale input must be dropped, nothing else may be over the island, keys need the page's focus inside it, and the sound rule must be stated.

## Decision
**1. One encoder, one picture, cropped in the running pipeline.** `desk-stream` puts `videocrop` ahead of its scaler (in passthrough, at no cost, with no island) and takes a new line from `desk-rtc`: `crop X Y W H OUTW OUTH` or `crop none`.
- The rectangle is clipped to the screen, onto even pixels and at least 32 a side. The picture is it scaled to fit `OUTW` x `OUTH`, aspect kept, never up; a level's or a contention step's lines cap it too.
- **A new picture size costs one keyframe**, because x264 reopens on new caps, as a `level` already does. **A rectangle that only moved costs nothing:** the next frame shows it. No pipeline is rebuilt, the payloader and its SSRC stay, and sequence numbers run on.
- Sound is the desk's, unchanged, on its own track, timestamped on the same capture clock (ADR 0037), so cropping can't move it against the picture.
- Every viewing session gets the island: the crop is the desk's. `deskd` sets one only while every `live` session wants it.

**2. Input carries the island's epoch.** `deskd` names each island with an epoch, 1 to 255, in `stream.crop` on `rtc.sock`; the app puts it in byte 3 of every input event (0 is the whole screen, which older apps already send). `desk-rtc` maps an event's normalized point into the island's clipped rectangle with the same rule `desk-stream` crops by (one test table for both), and drops anything for another island or for the whole screen while an island streams. Releases of what a session holds always go, so nothing stays down when an island ends.

**3. Keys only while the page's focus is in the island, and X sends them to the page.** `stream.crop` carries `keys`; `deskd` sets it only while the deep active element is inside the island's element. From `desk-rtc` 0.3.7 (T47S-1), a key down also needs X to send it to the kiosk's window: the focus in that window, or, as on `:99` after a `window.open` window closes (the focus falls back to the root and Chromium doesn't take it back), the focus on the root or PointerRoot with the kiosk's window under the pointer. A key with the focus on any other window, a popup that took it, is refused like a covered press (`islandBlocked`, `island.blocked`); a held key's release still goes.

**4. A press needs the kiosk's own window under it, and nothing over the island.** On `:99` there's no window manager: the kiosk's window covers the screen, and Chromium's popups, bubbles, menus and `window.open` windows are top-level windows of their own, each mapped above it. `desk-rtc` finds the kiosk's window by what a page can't set: `WM_CLASS` class `Chromium` and `WM_WINDOW_ROLE` `browser` (a `window.open` window's role is `pop-up`; menus, bubbles and a select's list are override-redirect), mapped, the lowest such in the stacking order. On each press in an island it lets the press through only if that window is the top-level window at the pixel, holds the whole island, and no mapped window stacked above it crosses the island, however large. **0.3.6 asked only whether the window at the pixel held the whole island** (Security's T47S-1, Medium): a select's popup bigger than a small island, or a screen-sized `window.open` opened on the first press of a double-click, passed it and got the member's next press. A refused press is counted (`islandBlocked`), its release goes nowhere, and `deskd` hears `island.blocked {epoch}`, at most once a second, so it can show the last frame and offer the full page. A press beside a popup that crosses the island is refused too, so `deskd` ends the island on `island.blocked`. JavaScript dialogs and tab-modal prompts are inside the page's window, so they're `deskd`'s to catch over CDP (`desk-runtime.md` §8.8).

**5. Starting and stopping are the states that exist.** An island in view in a visible Browser window is `live`; out of view or hidden is `warm`, keeping the crop, so coming back is one frame; no Browser window is `off`. **Sound follows the island:** it plays while the island is live and stops when it leaves the view, as `warm` always has.

## Results (one `c7g.xlarge`, 2026-11-07; `spikes/desk-stream-islands/README.md`)
| One `c7g.xlarge`, a 1080p60 video page, 2 x 120 s per mode | Full screen 1920x1080 | Island 640x360 | Island 960x540 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `desk-stream`, % of a core | 57.9 | **20.0** | **36.6** |
| Chromium (playing the clip) | 74.7 | 74.3 | 74.5 |
| The desk's processes together | 138.5 | 100.2 | 117.1 |
| Stream and page fps, average (lowest 5 s) | 59.8 (58.4) | 59.8 (58.4) | 59.8 (58.4) |
| Key to frame at `desk-rtc`, p50 / p95 ms (118 keys) | 23.0 / 30.1 | 18.2 / 26.3 | 19.6 / 27.3 |
| Sound after picture at the desk, p50 ms (240 events) | 20.8 | 25.0 | 19.6 |
| Mbit/s | 5.09 | 1.57 | 2.68 |

- **Crop change to the first good frame** (70 changes while playing): a new picture size 12 to 21 ms p50, 28 ms at worst, one keyframe each; a move 8 ms p50 with no keyframe. One SSRC and no RTP sequence gap throughout.
- **A/V sync:** the six runs' p50 spread from 19.1 to 27.1 ms, as much within a mode as between modes. Both tracks are timestamped at capture, ahead of the crop.
- **`warm`:** no frames, no sound, `desk-stream` at 0.1% of a core. **`off`:** no process. Live again at once.
- **Input through the real `desk-rtc`** (`keyproof`'s `island` phase): every click landed on the exact pixel its picture point maps to, including after the island moved; stale epochs and early keys were dropped; a press on a `<select>`'s popup over the island was refused and `deskd` heard `island.blocked`.
- Spend about $0.07, everything terminated.
- **T47S-1, `desk-rtc` 0.3.7** (one `c7g.xlarge` from `ma-desk-0.2.9`, real kiosk Chromium 156, `keyproof`'s island phase, 0 problems): a press on a select's popup bigger than a 64x64 island inside it, refused, nothing selected; a press beside the popup in the island, refused; a screen-sized `window.open` (1919x1079, role `pop-up`, which took the X focus) opened by the first press, the second press refused and none reached it; a key with deskd's `keys` set while that window had the focus, refused; with the window closed, the focus back on the root, a press and a key reached the page. `go test -race` with Xvfb, all pass (213), the real-X test covering each case. `spikes/desk-stream-islands/results/island-cover-proof.json`.
- **On the staging test desk** (`c7g.large`, image 0.2.9, both releases from 0.3.1 and 0.3.5, no island set, since `deskd` doesn't send one yet): all of `e2e/` 23 passed, 1 skipped; input to screen 62.2 / 75.9 ms idle and 94.0 / 108.7 loaded at about 55 ms RTT (120 trials each); `av-sync` passed twice, `desk-stream` 31.9 and 26.7% of a core while the clip plays against 0.3.1's 26.6, desk page 59.5 and 59.9 fps, desk-side sound against picture p50 0.6 and -5.6 ms. With no island, the passthrough `videocrop` costs nothing measurable. Unit tests on the merged branch in the box's real GStreamer: islands 6 of 6, levels 7 of 7, damage 17 of 17; `desk-rtc` `go test -race` with Xvfb, all pass (`docs/sub-status/desk-stream-islands.md`).

## Consequences
- A member watching a video in the simplified view costs the host what's in the table above, against a full-screen stream of the same page; the rest of the page renders on the member's own machine.
- deskd's part is real work: the rectangle from the element's box over CDP, sent on scroll and resize, the epoch, the focus watch, keeping the element in view, and ending the island on a dialog, fullscreen, navigation or another tab (`desk-runtime.md` §8.8).
- An old app with a new `desk-rtc` sees nothing new: it never asks for an island, and byte 3 is 0. A new app with an old `desk-rtc` (0.3.5 or before) gets no island: `deskd` mustn't offer one, since that `desk-rtc` ignores `stream.crop` and would map island input onto the whole screen. Nor with 0.3.6, whose press check a window bigger than the island passes (T47S-1).
- **Open:** whether a video the member is listening to should keep playing when its island scrolls out of view, as ADR 0038 keeps a hidden tab's sound (the owner's or the Product Designer's call; the cost is the island's encode while it's off screen). `deskcap` still makes frames for damage outside the island, each a cheap repeat; a damage filter for the island's rectangle would trim that. Two islands at once, and islands on member Guild Desks (no encoder), aren't built.
