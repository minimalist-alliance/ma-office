## Context
Task 47, the owner: "id still like to see videos in minified mode and relevant content other than spam and ads". Task 47 asks for a maintained blocklist in the desk's Chromium in both the streamed view and the simplified view, with a per-site switch for when a site breaks, controllable by the member from the office. Task 46, the owner's top priority, wants CPU and bandwidth back, and every page's ads add to both.

Bounds: nothing may clear or lose a member's session, history, cookies or extensions (ADR 0035); no list or filter may send browsing data anywhere beyond what Chromium already sends; a teen's desk runs with `ExtensionInstallBlocklist: ["*"]` (the teen lock); the Agent's filter (ADR 0070) owns uid 1103's DNS and must not be touched. The desk is Chromium 156 (Debian's `156.0.8078.79`) on arm64, a `c7g`, with no GPU.

The Security Analyst's requirements (`security-analyst.md`): `T47-8`, the blocklist is network blocking and hiding only, no scriptlets, rewrites or cookie rules, compiled at MA from pinned lists, signed. `T47S-2`, against the first build (uBlock Origin Lite itself from the Web Store, unpinned, in its default mode with page scripts and the cookie-notice list): its scriptlets can remove and set cookies and site storage, click for the member and rewrite responses, chosen weekly by its author and unreviewed, which can break ADR 0035, and a hijacked release would run on every page of every desk. Fix: a pinned version from an MA update URL, updated by a reviewed diff; network blocking only; a `verify.sh` check that cookies and local storage are byte-identical after pages with the blocker on, on every version; a kill switch that doesn't uninstall. `T47S-3`: the per-site list was in a policy file every desk user could read, and clearing history didn't clear it.

**The owner's standing rule (2026-11-08):** finished features ship on, in prod and on the owner's desk; switches are kill switches, never opt-ins. So blocking is on for every desk, adult or teen, from the image that carries it, and the only switches are the member's per-site off list and the Platform's kill switch.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Kill switch without uninstalling** | The policy's `off`: every list off, the extension kept | Its `defaultFiltering`, with the same race at a start | Nothing to kill | Turn the resolver off |

Tried and dropped on the way to the chosen one, each measured on a lab box with the teen lock in place:
- **uBOL's network-only mode by policy** (`defaultFiltering: "basic"`): uBOL reads its managed settings through a copy it caches, reads Chromium's in the background, and doesn't listen for changes (`admin.js`, `adminReadEx`). At a start of Chromium it can read before Chromium has filled them, and then it stays in its default mode, with its scriptlets registered for `<all_urls>`, until it restarts. Seen on a fresh profile and on the restart after it. So the mode can't be relied on.
- **Keeping uBOL off pages with Chromium's `runtime_blocked_hosts: ["*://*"]`**: its scripts stay out, and so does its blocking: Chromium stops `declarativeNetRequest` for an extension on blocked hosts. Blocking nothing.
- **Withholding uBOL's site access** (`developerPrivate.updateExtensionConfiguration`): "Cannot change host permissions for the given extension", for any policy-installed extension.
- **`allowAllRequests` for the per-site switch**: Chromium applies it only where the extension has host permissions, and ours has none. So a site off is two `allow` rules instead.

## Decision
**The image builds its own blocker** at bake time with `image/recipe/adblock/build-blocker.py`, from uBlock Origin Lite's GitHub release for Chromium at the version and sha256 in `image/recipe/adblock/blocker.pin` (GPLv3; the lists under their own licences, noted in the extension's `NOTICE.txt`). It keeps the default lists' `block` and `allow` rules and drops their `redirect` (881) and `modifyHeaders` (52) rules, and none of uBOL's scripts. The extension ("Minimalist Alliance blocker") is those rules, `worker.js` and `managed_storage.json` from `image/recipe/adblock`, with the permissions `declarativeNetRequest` and `storage` and nothing else. It's signed as a CRX3 with an RSA key made in that step and destroyed in it, so each image has its own extension id; a new image reinstalls it, which loses nothing, since it keeps nothing of the member's.

- **The policy** comes from the image's template `/etc/ma/chromium-adblock.json`, which shares no key with `ma.json` or `chromium-teen.json` (`build.sh`, the unit tests and `verify.sh` refuse a shared one). `build.sh` writes the extension's id into it:
  - `ExtensionSettings`: the blocker `force_installed` from `file:///usr/share/ma/adblock/update.xml`, with `override_update_url`, so Chromium installs it from the CRX in the image and updates it from nowhere else. A new version is a reviewed change to `blocker.pin` (`build-blocker.py diff` lists every rule added and removed), a bake and `verify.sh`.
  - `DeveloperToolsAvailability: 1`, so the desk's own trusted processes can read the blocker's state over port 9222. Unset, Chromium refuses DevTools on a force-installed extension and nowhere else, so this only adds the blocker.
  - The blocker's settings as `3rdparty` policy: `noFiltering`, the member's list, and `off`, the kill switch.
- **The worker** applies the policy: every list on unless `off`, and for the listed sites two `allow` rules at the top priority, one for every request a listed site's pages make (`initiatorDomains`) and one for the site's own pages and frames. It applies at install, at every start of Chromium, and whenever Chromium says the managed settings changed (`chrome.storage.onChanged`, area `managed`). So a list changed while Chromium was down applies by itself a moment after the start, and so does a kill switch. A frame from elsewhere inside a page that's off still has its own requests filtered: the price of no host permissions.
- **Both views.** The simplified view is built from the same real tab in the same Chromium (design §7.2), so what the blocker stops never reaches it. Without cosmetic hiding, an ad's empty box can stay in the page; the walker drops frames that loaded nothing.
- **The per-site switch.** `deskd` owns `/data/desk/adblock-off`, one site a line. The root helper `desk-adblock` turns it into the policy:
  - `render`, before every start of Chromium (`desk-browser.service`, `ExecStartPre=-+`), so restarts, crash restarts and a new root take the list from `/data`. A failure leaves the policy that's there and never stops the Browser.
  - `apply`, on demand: `deskd` asks `start desk-adblock` on `units.sock` after it writes the list. As root it writes the policy; then, as uid 1100, it has Chromium read its policy files at once (`chrome://policy`'s reload, in a hidden page, which is no tab and never reaches history or the session) and reads the blocker's state back from its service worker until the policy, what the worker says it applied, its rules and its lists all match, steady for 1.5 s. Then it answers (about 2 s measured), and `deskd` reloads the tab. A CDP call that isn't answered in 4 s costs a new connection, not the apply.
  - The interface for the Desk and UI/UX Engineers is `docs/interfaces/desk-runtime.md` §8.9.
- **The kill switch** is `/data/.ma-adblock-off`, a regular file root owns. With it there, `render` sets `off`, and the blocker turns every list off and stays installed with the member's list kept. The Platform sets it on a desk with `touch /data/.ma-adblock-off` and `systemctl start desk-adblock` (SSM), and takes it away the same way. It's on `/data`, so a root swap keeps it; uid 1100 can't make or remove it. On a desk whose Chromium is frozen (ADR 0045), `systemctl start desk-adblock` can't confirm and fails after 20 s, with the policy written: thaw it first (`deskd`'s `start desk-adblock` on `units.sock` does), or it applies at Chromium's next start at the latest. That Chromium picks it up at a thaw on its own isn't measured.
- **`T47S-3`:** the policy is `root:kiosk 0640`: Chromium reads it, and no other user on the desk can see the list (uids 1100 and 1103 measured refused). The list's one source is `deskd`'s file: every start renders the policy from it, so a clear of history, in which `deskd` empties the list and calls `start desk-adblock` (the Desk Engineer's half, on `sub/minified-stage-a`), drops every entry from the policy at once and from every start after. The list is in the member's export as part of "Desk settings and Chat" (`/data/desk`); the kill switch isn't, being the Platform's.
- **Site exceptions.** `image/recipe/adblock/site-allow.json` is a short list of `allow` rules for pages that break or get slower because the blocker is there: `{sites, urlFilter, why}`, meaning the pages of those sites, and no others, may fetch one request, `||host/path`. `build-blocker.py` builds it into a ruleset of the blocker, `ma-site-allow`, at priority 100,000 (uBOL's own rules are at 10, 30 and 40; the member's per-site switch at 1,000,000), and refuses any entry that isn't that: a bare host, a wildcard, no site, no `why`, or any other key. It's the same rule type the lists already use for their own per-site exceptions (about 2,900 of uBOL's rules are `allow` rules at priority 30), so nothing new reaches the Security Analyst's `T47S-2`: no redirect, no header rule, no script. The kill switch turns it off with the rest. Entries come from a trace (`image/lab/adblock/bench/trace.py`) and a bisect of the hosts a page has blocked (`ddmin.py`, `ddmin2.py`), and each says why. See "Site exceptions" under "Measured".
- **It's on the safe side when it fails:** an unreadable list is blocking everywhere, a bad line is dropped, a kill switch that isn't a root-owned regular file is ignored, and a missing template leaves the last policy. It logs counts, never a site.

## Measured
**How.** One `c7g.xlarge` (4 vCPU) from `ma-desk-0.2.9`, tagged `Purpose=browser-benchmark`, `Owner=ma-task47-d`, with `deskd`, `desk-rtc`, `desk-agent` and `desk-notebook` stopped so nothing else used the CPU or the network. Chromium 156 started by the image's own `desk-browser-start` (the desk's flags, `--disable-gpu`, the 1920x1080 window on display `:99`, sound to `desk-audio`) in a unit of its own, so its cgroup counts every Chromium process. Each pass is one config on a fresh profile in a fresh Chromium, 13 sites in a row, one tab at a time; 3 passes per config with the order rotated; medians of the 3. Per site: load (to the load event, at most 30 s, then 3 s); a click on "Agree" or "Accept" when a modal locked the page, as a member would, in every config alike (only CNN's terms needed one); 20 s of reading with no input; the simplified view's prototype extractor (`spikes/browser-bench/minified-proto`) once; then 12 s of wheel scrolling at about 1,200 px a second while the page's own `requestAnimationFrame` is counted. **CPU** is the whole Chromium cgroup over the visit, in CPU-seconds; **bytes** are what `ens5` received over the visit; **memory** is the PSS of every Chromium process after reading; **page fps** is the average and the worst whole second while scrolling. Harness: `image/lab/adblock/bench/`.

Two runs, each on a box of its own, no errors in either:
- **The first build**, 117 visits: no blocker, uBOL with its defaults, and uBOL with its cookie-notice and overlay lists. Raw: `evidence/adblock-2026-11-07/visits.jsonl`, `tables.md`, `screens-news.jpg`, `screens-other.jpg`.
- **What ships**, 78 visits: no blocker, and the image's own blocker as a desk gets it (built by `build-blocker.py` from the pin, its policy rendered by `desk-adblock` from the template, no site off). Raw: `visits-shipped.jsonl`, `tables-shipped.md`, `screens-shipped-news.jpg`, `screens-shipped-other.jpg` in the same folder.

**Why allow rules, and no `redirect`.** allrecipes' video was suspected to need one of the 881 dropped `redirect` rules, a harmless stand-in for a script. It doesn't: with the real script allowed for those sites alone, the player loads and plays. Nothing here needs a redirect, so none is added: a new rule type is the Security Analyst's to clear.

**What each exception costs.** A page may load a library it would load with no blocker: Google Publisher Tag on Yahoo, AdSense's script and Criteo's prebid adapter on allrecipes and Fandom, Connatix's player on Tasty. The ad requests those libraries then make stay blocked: after the exceptions Yahoo still has `securepubads.g.doubleclick.net` blocked 2 times, and allrecipes and Fandom have it blocked for `gpt.js` itself (it isn't allowed there). The libraries can't read or set anything a page's own scripts couldn't, and they could on that site with no blocker. **For the Security Analyst:** these are `allow` rules, the type the lists use for their own exceptions; three request-level entries for named sites, one for named sites and a prefix.

## What it can't do
- **Sites that refuse a cloud address** refuse it with or without blocking: from the measuring box, YouTube asked to sign in "to confirm you're not a bot", Reddit said "You've been blocked by network security" and Fandom showed a Cloudflare check on one pass. Blocking doesn't change that, and neither does the switch.
- **Ads served from the site's own paths**, anti-adblock walls, and what uBOL fixes with redirects and scriptlets, which this doesn't carry. **Pages that test whether an ad library loaded** can get slower or break their own player: Yahoo, Fandom, allrecipes and Tasty did, and each is fixed with an exception. The next one isn't known until someone sees it: 3 of the 23 sites in the wider check had a difference, and all three were an ad. The member's switch is the way out, a site at a time, and a new exception is a reviewed line of `site-allow.json`. A fix that only a `redirect` would give isn't made here.
- **Hiding.** No cosmetic filters, so an ad's empty space can stay. The simplified view drops it.
- **A frame from elsewhere inside a site that's off** keeps its own requests filtered (no host permissions, so `allow` rules, not `allowAllRequests`).
- **A new desk's first seconds.** The extension installs from the image on the Browser's first start, in about 1.6 s measured; pages before then aren't filtered.
- **Updates.** Lists go stale between pins. A pin is a reviewed diff and a bake; nothing updates the blocker in between.

## Consequences
- **Every image carries the blocker** at the pinned version (`/usr/share/ma/adblock`: the CRX, its update manifest, its id and `blocker.json`, what went in), about 9 MB less than uBOL with its scripts. The bake fetches the release from GitHub and refuses one whose sha256 isn't the pin's. Self-hosted desks build it the same way in `selfhost/install.sh`.
- **A new image is a new extension id**, so a root swap reinstalls the blocker (it keeps nothing of the member's). If that churn ever matters, the Platform can keep one signing key and `build-blocker.py` can take it.
- **The `install` part also checks the site exceptions** (the installed `ma-site-allow` is the build's, each rule an `allow` for named sites and one request, and it is one of the blocker's rulesets); passed on `ma-desk-test-adsites-1`.
- **`verify.sh` gains nine checks**: eight parts in section 3b5 (`install`, `switch`, `restart`, `stopped`, `kill`, `cookies`, `hostile`, `keys`) and one in 6c. `cookies` is `T47S-2`'s: a profile's cookies of every kind and its local storage are byte-identical after pages with the blocker on, a site off and on again, and no script of the blocker is in a page. Each part was seen to fail with what it guards broken: `image/evidence/adblock-2026-11-07/breaks.txt` on what ships, `breaks-swap.txt` (6c) on the first build only.
- **`deskd` and the Desk app** build the switch: `docs/interfaces/desk-runtime.md` §8.9. On an older image `start desk-adblock` is `err denied unit not allowed`, and the switch should hide.
- **Removing the force-install later uninstalls the blocker** from every desk, with its own storage, which holds only what it applied.
- **The Security Analyst** reads `DeveloperToolsAvailability: 1` (it reaches only the blocker, our own code) and this revision against `T47S-2` and `T47S-3` before it goes to members.
