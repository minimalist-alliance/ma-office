# ADR 0048: Files app

A Dropbox-like window onto the desk's own drive.

**Status:** Accepted (Lead decisions, 2026-11-08). Built on `sub/files-app` (task "files-app", story S-117). The exact wire is `services/deskd/docs/files.md`; this page is the decisions, the reasons and the work split.
**Builds on:** the export draft (`draft-data-export-and-transfer.md`: the `bulk` read path, CREDIT/CHUNK/END, the `files` part), ADR 0035 (browser session; the 2 GiB floor), 0011 (desk data), 0013 and 0026 (idle), 0031 (waking), 0045 (frozen), the desk app contract draft, and `ma-platform` `docs/contracts/image-check.md` (the image check, read 2026-11-08).

## Context
The owner: the drive is "for all data from all apps"; a Files app like Dropbox, uploads and downloads, on the desk's own drive (32 GB default). The rules this keeps: the platform sees no desk content (README, ADR 0011), nothing of ours stores file bytes, a desk's browser session is never touched (ADR 0035), a member reaches only their own desk (deskd's token check: `aud` is the desk, `sub` its owner, `owner` scope). the owner's image rule, 2026-11-08: every image uploaded to or through MA is checked by the shared image-check service as the last upload step; the member sees their place in line, never "busy"; if the checker is offline, images go unchecked.

## Decisions

### 1. Files live in `/data/files`, owned by `desk`, `0700`
deskd (uid 1100) reads and writes it in-process: no root helper, no widening of what deskd can reach beyond one directory. The Browser profile, apps' data, `rtc`, `selfhost`, `desk` etc. are siblings of it, so Files can't list, read or delete them by construction. Inside: `.uploads/` (partials and their sidecars) and `.trash/` (deleted items, 30 days, counted in the quota). The export already lists `files` as a part (its section 4); `.uploads` is added to what it leaves out (see "What this asks of others").

**Two corrections to the brief, from the code:**
- **deskd's container does not see `/data/files`.** It runs `--read-only` with `/data/desk` the only mount of `/data` (`desk-runtime.md` section 5, the `docker run` line). Files needs one more bind mount, `-v /data/files:/data/files`, and the directory made at boot (`0700`, uid 1100, with `desk-datadirs`). That is an image change, the System Engineer's, and the main thing that gates a desk-side test. It is a mount of one directory, not a widening of deskd's reach over the volume.
- **Other apps do not see the files "as plain files".** The app contract draft gives an app `TemporaryFileSystem=/data:ro` with only its own directories bound back (section 4 table: "the desk's storage drive ... Only a file the member picks, each time: `files.pick`, then `files.read` and `files.write` on that handle"). The contract's drive is this tree: when an app is allowed a file, deskd serves it from `/data/files` through the handle. No ambient access, which is also the privacy line. The `files.` namespace is reserved for deskd there; the app-side `files.read` and `files.write` (app to deskd, on `app.sock`) are a different dispatcher from the client's `files.read` here, but a reader must not confuse them, and the Desk Engineer names the app-side ones differently (`app.files.*`) before 1.0, or this is documented in both.

### 2. Path safety: deskd is the only writer, and paths are checked twice
- **Grammar** (files.md section 0): relative to the root, `/`-separated, NFC (refused if not, never rewritten), no `..`, no NUL or other control character, no backslash, no bidi override, no empty or `.` component, a name at most 255 bytes, a path at most 1024. Reserved at the root: `.uploads`, `.trash`. Case-sensitive; a rename or upload that would overwrite is `exists` unless `replace: true`.
- **Resolution:** deskd opens the root directory once and walks every component with `SecureDirectoryStream` (`openat`, `unlinkat`, `renameat`; `NOFOLLOW_LINKS` on each step), so a component swapped for a symlink between the check and the use fails instead of escaping. At start deskd checks the filesystem returns a `SecureDirectoryStream` and the tree is `0700` uid 1100; if not, every `files.*` answers `unavailable`, `{reason: "tree"}`.
- **Symlinks** are never created, followed or listed. Neither are devices, FIFOs, sockets, files with `nlink` over 1 (a hard link into the tree could only come from root), or names that aren't valid NFC UTF-8: all are skipped and counted in `list`'s `skipped`.
- **TOCTOU:** nothing but deskd writes the tree (mode `0700`, apps excluded by the contract), and deskd's own namespace changes run under one lock, so "does the target exist" and "rename" can't interleave, including across the member's two tabs. For defence in depth every resolved path is also checked to still be under the real root (`toRealPath` of the parent) before a destructive step. Reads open the file once with no-follow and read that handle.
- **Errors never name a file** (`details` carry codes only), because log lines and ERR bodies travel.

### 3. The quota is the whole drive's
The owner: the drive is for all apps. So the Files app shows `statvfs` of `/data` (used, free, total: `files.usage`) plus how much Files itself holds and how much is in the trash ("Files 4.1 GB, Trash 0.3 GB, all other apps 9.0 GB, 18.6 GB free"). Uploads are refused when they would take free space under the **2 GiB floor** of ADR 0035 (the export draft's section 5 uses the same): at `begin` against `free - floor - bytes still owed to running uploads` (so two uploads can't both pass), and again as bytes arrive, every 8 MiB or 5 s, because Chromium and apps grow the same drive. `no_space` carries the three numbers; the window says "Not enough room: 2.3 GB free, 2 GiB is kept for your Browser" and offers the trash. The trash is never emptied early to make room; only the member empties it. Files never touches `/data/browser`, so ADR 0035's "caches only" rule holds.

### 4. Transfers go over the desk's own `bulk` channel: nothing of ours sees the bytes
Downloads reuse the export's frames unchanged (`files.read` = `data.read` with in-process reads). Uploads are the new direction on the same channel; the design is files.md section 5. In short: `upload.begin` makes a partial `.uploads/<uploadId>.part` and grants a 1 MiB window; the browser sends CHUNK frames (kind 15, BIN, `id` = the session's `transferId`) within credit; deskd answers CREDIT (kind 16, JSON) `{bytes, committed}` as it writes; `upload.status` resumes by returning the committed offset (a lost tab, a lost session, a restarted deskd); `upload.finish` waits for the last byte, checks the SHA-256 against what is on disk, runs the image gate, `fsync`s and renames the partial into place, so no half file is ever visible. Partials with no progress for 24 h are removed. Not presigned S3: that would put member content in our storage.

**Does `desk-rtc` or the gateway need a change? No (checked in code).** The browser already opens `bulk` as a bidirectional, reliable, ordered channel (design section 4.2.3, `peer.ts`'s `BULK`), `desk-rtc` bridges client frames on every channel 0 to 5 to deskd under per-channel credit (`session.go`, `rx[channel].acquire`, 256 KiB each way, SCTP receive window 4 MiB), the gateway carries only signalling, and deskd's `Session.route` hands any `bulk` frame to the registered handler (today `Transfers`, which drops everything but CREDIT). No new frame kind: CHUNK client to desk, CREDIT desk to client (the export already uses both kinds, in the other direction). What changes is deskd (`Transfers`/a new `Uploads` handler) and the web's `BulkChannel` (a `send` and a CREDIT listener, several listeners keyed by id; today one `onChunk` serves the export page).

**The main risks, in order:**
1. **Client-to-desk throughput over `bulk` is unmeasured.** The export measured desk-to-client (35.5, 8.8 and 4.5 Mbit/s at 10, 50 and 100 ms, limited by Pion's cwnd at the sender); the sender here is Chrome's SCTP, with a different limit, and the receiver is Pion's 4 MiB window. Builder A's first task is a spike (real `desk-rtc` + `fakedeskd`, with `tc netem` for 10, 50 and 100 ms) that fails the plan if uploads run under 8 Mbit/s at 50 ms; if so the fix is in `desk-rtc` (the System Engineer) and everything else is unchanged.
2. **One channel for both directions.** A slow disk must not hold up the same session's downloads and CREDITs, because `desk-rtc` gets credit back only when deskd's handler returns. So the handler only queues to the transfer's writer (bounded by the 1 MiB window) and returns; the disk is touched on the writer's own thread (files.md 5.2).
3. **Download speed while the Browser is on screen.** `desk-rtc` caps `bulk` at about 2 Mbit/s while a session views (design 4.2.6). A download with the Browser window open is slow. The Files window says so ("Downloads are slower while your Browser is on screen") and the cap is not changed: video comes first. Uploads (client to desk) are unaffected.

**Money:** uploads cost the member's bandwidth nothing (the meter counts what the desk sends). Downloads through an `owner` session count like any byte the desk sends, and while a member is paused at the bandwidth limit `files.read` answers `unavailable`, `bandwidth_limit` (as `data.read` does), and the window says when it resumes and points to "Download your data", which is free and never refused (the export). Folder downloads are many `files.read`s and count the same.

### 5. Methods (owner scope only; `export` never gets write methods, or any `files.*`)
`files.list`, `stat`, `mkdir`, `rename` (rename and move; `replace` sends the old file to the trash), `delete` (to the trash; `permanent` only for `trash:` ids), `trash.list`, `trash.restore`, `trash.empty`, `search`, `usage`, `upload.begin/status/finish/abort`, `read`, `read.stop`, and the topic `files.changes`. Lists page at 2000, sorted folders first then case-folded name. Search is by name, substring, case-insensitive, capped at 200 results and 200,000 entries or 3 s. The `export` scope stays as it is (`data.*` only): a closed account's export already carries the `files` part, so it can't write and doesn't need `files.*`.

### 6. Limits
No fixed per-file cap beyond free space minus the floor. In the browser: at most 3 files uploading at once and 8 transfers per session (the deskd limit, shared with `data.read`; 12 per desk). Folder upload from the directory picker (`webkitdirectory`) and from drag-and-drop (`DataTransferItem.webkitGetAsEntry`); missing folders are made at `finish` so no per-folder call is needed. Progress per transfer with speed and time left (reuse `export/eta.ts`). A folder download is a **ZIP made in the member's browser**: reuse `web/src/export/zip.ts` (`ZipWriter`, STORED entries, ZIP64) and the sink (`sink.ts`: save-file picker, or the OPFS working copy, as the export does), fetching each file with `files.read` (ordered by a breadth-first `files.list` walk). A single file downloads directly through the same sink. The folder zip resumes within a session (a dropped link resumes the current file from its offset, as `copier.ts` does) but is not kept across a page reload in v1. No previews in v1: files are untrusted bytes (design 4.2.13); icons come from the extension and decide nothing.

### 7. A stopped, frozen or held desk
- **"Frozen" is a different thing from what the brief guessed.** In this code (ADR 0045) it is Chromium's cgroup freeze on a desk nobody looks at. `files.*` never reaches Chromium (as `data.read` doesn't), so a frozen desk serves Files normally, never thaws and never keeps the freeze off. There is no read-only `frozen` state and no `frozen` error.
- **A lapsed or held membership** shows as `vm: 'held'` and the platform refuses `connect`, so there is no session at all. The Files window shows the existing membership panel (`MEMBERSHIP_LINE`, the billing link), as every app does; data-download rights are served by the export (`export` token, `data.*`), which the panel points to. Nothing in Files needs to work without an owner session.
- **Stopped (asleep):** Files needs deskd. It shows the same asleep panel as the Browser and Agent windows (`Waking`, `WakeBar`, `wakeDesk`, ADR 0031) with "Wake your desk". The window keeps the list it last showed, dimmed and read-only, until the session opens; a transfer in progress when a session drops pauses ("Waiting for your desk") and resumes by itself (uploads via `status`, downloads from the offset).
- **Idle-stop (ADR 0026):** the sweeper counts an open session as a client being present, and a session is closed by the browser only after 60 min hidden or 24 h with no input (`idle.ts`). Transfers are in ADR 0026's list of activity ("file transfers"), but nothing measures them today: `lastInputAt` comes only from `ACTIVITY` frames and desk-rtc's input. So two changes: (a) deskd notes input (`SessionManager.noteInput`) from an owner session's upload bytes and `files.read` bytes, at most once a minute; (b) `IdleRule` gets a `busy(deskId)` hook that the Files transfers answer, so a hidden tab with a running transfer keeps its session open instead of resting after 60 min. A paused transfer on a closed tab is not activity.

### 8. Images: sniffed by content, checked by the platform, verified by the desk
**Aligned to the real contract** (`image-check.md`, read in `sub/image-check`), which differs from the brief in four ways, each handled here:
1. **The service takes `image/jpeg`, `image/png`, `image/webp` only, at most 10 MiB, static**, and returns a **stripped JPEG** (EXIF removed, re-encoded; "the original is never kept"). gif, heic/heif, avif, bmp, tiff and svg are `failed` (`bad_type`, `animated`), and a large phone photo may be over 10 MiB.
2. **There is no verdict token in the contract.** The browser uploads to the service with a presigned PUT, polls `GET /v1/image-check/{imageId}` for `place`, then fetches the stripped copy from `GET .../url` and `DELETE`s the service's copy after saving its own (contract section 2.3, "the Files app on the desk").
3. `unchecked` (checker offline) is final and is treated as `ok`.
4. The service can say `rejected` with `code` G1 to G6, `guideline` and `message`.

**Decision: what lands on the desk for an image is the stripped JPEG the service returns, never the original, and only with a verdict token.**
- **Browser flow:** sniff the file (magic bytes; SVG by root element) before anything else. Not an image: no check, plain upload. An image: `ImageCheck.check(file)` (web interface below) → events → on `passed`, the browser holds the stripped bytes and a token. It uploads those bytes (named `<stem>.jpg`) with `files.upload.begin {token}` and `finish {token}`, then `settle(imageId)` (DELETE). The member sees "Checking your image: 3rd in line." from the queue, never "busy", and the check is the last step before the desk upload.
- **The token (asked of the platform, section "What this asks of others"):** an ES256 JWS from the platform, `typ: ma-image-verdict+jwt`, signed with the key whose JWKS deskd already caches for desk tokens, bound to the desk (`aud`), the member (`sub`), the exact bytes (`ma_sha256`, `ma_bytes`), `ma_state` (`ok` or `unchecked`), `ma_feature: files`, 30 minutes. It carries no `scope`, so a verdict can never pass `DeskTokenVerifier` as a desk token. deskd finalizes an image into the tree only when the token verifies and matches the SHA-256 it computed itself: `check_required` (an image with no token) and `check_invalid` (files.md section 6).
- **On a fail:** `rejected` shows the guideline in plain words with its code ("This image breaks guideline G2 ... so it wasn't added. Read the guidelines.") and nothing lands on the drive; the service has already deleted it. `failed` shows the reason in plain words (too large, not a supported type, animated). `error` (network, `429 rate_limited` with `retryAfterSeconds`) offers retry; the file isn't added.
- **Unsupported image types are refused, not passed through** (fail closed, the owner's rule "every image"): gif, heic/heif, avif, bmp, tiff and svg, and any image over the service's `maxBytes`. The window says which kinds work and that the file can be converted. **Asked of the platform:** raise `maxBytes` for feature `files` to 64 MiB (a phone photo is routinely over 10), and take gif (static), heic/heif, avif, bmp and tiff; svg is not a bitmap and stays refused unless the owner decides otherwise. Until then these limits are real, and the `ImageCheck` interface's `supports(format, bytes)` is how the window knows before it uploads.
- **What it does to the member's files, said plainly:** a PNG screenshot or a photo added through Files is stored as a JPEG with no EXIF (no location, no camera, no colour profile). That is the contract's open question 7.1 ("originals ... a separate decision"), and **it is the owner's call**: the alternative is a service that checks and returns only a verdict for the original bytes, which changes the token's hash to the original's and needs no other change here (deskd only compares `ma_sha256` with what it received).
- **Archives and documents upload unchecked (Lead decision):** zip, 7z, rar, tar and every other archive are plain uploads, and Files never unpacks one to look inside. Documents with images inside them, such as PDFs, pass unchecked too. Whether a file is an image is decided only by its content (magic bytes; SVG by root element), never by its name, so an image renamed `.dat` or `.txt` still goes through the check.
- **Limits of the check, stated:** images inside zips or any non-image file are not checked; images made by other apps on the desk (a Browser download, an agent's output) are not "uploaded through MA" and are not checked; an image already on the drive is never rechecked.
- **Privacy line (public wording is the owner's call):** every other file stays on the desk and between the desk and the member. An image added through Files is sent to MA's image check, seen once by the check, and kept only until the check is done and the member's desk has its copy (the service then deletes it; if the check rejects it, at once); the service keeps the outcome, the code and an ID. This is a carve-out from ADR 0011's "the platform can't read desk content", made by the owner for images, member-initiated, in transit only. Confirm against the platform's retention (contract section 4.4, 2.3) before any public line; the Terms and Privacy pages carry no image-check promise today (Sales, 2026-11-08).
- **Web, until the platform has the token route:** the interface and a stub, so the app and its tests don't wait:
```ts
export type CheckEvent =
  | { type: 'queued'; position: number; words: string }              // contract: checking, place; "Checking your image: 3rd in line."
  | { type: 'checking'; words: string }                              // uploading, processing
  | { type: 'passed'; token: string; bytes: Blob; sha256: string; unchecked: boolean; imageId: string }
  | { type: 'rejected'; code: string; guideline: string; words: string; guidelinesUrl: string }   // G1 to G6
  | { type: 'refused'; reason: string; words: string }               // contract "failed": too_large, bad_type, ...
  | { type: 'error'; retryable: boolean; words: string };            // network, 429, 5xx
export interface ImageCheck {
  supports(format: string, bytes: number): { ok: true } | { ok: false; words: string };
  check(file: Blob, deskId: string, signal: AbortSignal): AsyncIterable<CheckEvent>;
  settle(imageId: string): Promise<void>;                            // DELETE the service's copy once the desk has its own
}
```
The stub (`StubImageCheck`, used by `MockDeskLink` and the dev build until the token route exists) plays queued 3, 2, 1, checking, passed, with a token `stub.<sha256>` that only a deskd started with `DESKD_FILES_IMAGE_GATE=stub` (the rig and test desks, never a release image) accepts, and has switches to reject (G2) and to fail. A build that carries it must not reach a member: the real `ImageCheck` is chosen by client-config's platform base, and the stub only when `import.meta.env.DEV` or the test harness sets it.

### 9. Guild desks: out of v1
Personal desks only. The Files entry is hidden on a guild desk and deskd answers `forbidden` there. Shared files on a guild's server need the permissions decision of `draft-guild-shared-desk` (and ADR 0080) first: who may read, write and delete what.

### 10. Never broken
A desk's browser session, history and cookies are never cleared for anyone, and Files can't reach them (they're outside `/data/files`; no method takes a path outside it). Files shows nothing outside the tree. A member reaches only their own desk: deskd's token check (`aud` = this desk, `sub` = its owner, `scope` owner, ES256) is the same gate, and no `files.*` call crosses it. There is no terminal, no shell and no SSH here, as the export draft promised.

### 11. The Web app
Registry entry in `web/src/apps/registry.tsx` (`files`, "Files", the lazy chunk once it's big), the folder `web/src/apps/files/`. The window follows the Desk app's design system (kit colors and Nunito, ADRs 0060 and 0065; light, dusk and dark) and its a11y rules (`docs/sub-status/a11y-desk.md`, `web/CLAUDE.md`): a keyboard-complete list (arrow keys, Home/End, Enter opens, Backspace goes up, Shift and Ctrl ranges, type-ahead, F2 renames, Delete moves to the trash, with single keys acting only while the window has focus), a live region for transfer starts and ends (polite) and a `progressbar` per transfer with its words, a dialog for every destructive step with focus returned to the row, "Move to..." as a folder picker (drag is never the only way), breadcrumbs as a `nav`, reduced-motion plain progress. Fixtures: `MockDeskLink` answers `files.*` from an in-memory tree (`mockDeskFiles.ts`) honoring files.md, so `npm run dev` works with files, a trash, a full-drive case and the image stub. Ships ON (no client-config flag); a kill switch only: `files.enabled` in client-config, default true, which hides the entry.

## Alternatives not taken
- **Presigned S3 for transfers:** puts member content in our storage and costs S3; the desk's own channel needs neither.
- **A root helper for Files:** the tree is the desk's own; a helper would widen nothing it needs and add a socket to keep.
- **Files over the whole `/data`:** one wrong click could delete the Browser's cookies (ADR 0035). The tree is the boundary.
- **Share a tree with apps as plain files:** breaks the app contract's isolation.
- **A token for the original bytes now:** not in the contract; the stripped JPEG is what exists. The token's design allows switching with no desk change.
- **New frame kinds for uploads (ACK, abort):** `desk-rtc` and the web would both change for nothing; CREDIT's JSON carries them.
- **Hash first, upload second:** a multi-GB file waits minutes before the first byte; the hash runs while sending, and deskd verifies at `finish`.
- **Auto-emptying the trash when space is short:** a surprise deletion; the member decides.

## Consequences
- A member has an ordinary file manager on their own drive, with nothing of ours holding the bytes. Images are the one exception, by the owner's rule.
- Files and the Browser share one drive. A full drive stops uploads at 2 GiB free, so the Browser's session is protected from Files.
- deskd gains a write path to the volume, narrowed to one directory; the image gains one mount.
- A large upload is bounded by the member's uplink and the `bulk` channel (to be measured). A 32 GB drive full of video is a long upload.
- Photos added through Files lose their EXIF and become JPEGs until the platform decides on originals.
- The `BulkChannel` interface in the web changes shape (several listeners, a sender), so the export page changes with it (tests cover both).

## Lead decisions, Nov 8 (these override anything above that says otherwise)
1. **The member's image is stored as the original, byte for byte**, as Dropbox does. The check gets its own downscaled copy; the member's file is never re-encoded or stripped. So the verdict token binds the SHA-256 of the **original** bytes, and the browser uploads the original to the desk. Forums and other public places keep their stripped copies.
2. **Privacy:** the platform sees a downscaled copy of images uploaded through Files, for the check only, and keeps nothing after the verdict. The owner ordered the check for Files, so this is the approved exception to ADR 0011. The Lead publishes the wording on /privacy.
3. **Files never refuses an image for its type or size**, only for quota (`no_space`). The image-check agent is making the service accept every common image type (heic, gif, avif, tiff, bmp, svg and more) at any size, converting and downscaling in AWS before the model sees it. Build against the contract as if it accepts them; remove the jpeg/png/webp and 10 MiB limits from the design.
4. **Verdict token:** the platform adds a signed verdict token to the check contract (bound to SHA-256 and desk id, as designed).
5. **`/data/files` mount and boot-time directory:** made in the image recipe on this branch (`image/recipe`); the System Engineer includes it in the next bake.
