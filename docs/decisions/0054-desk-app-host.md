# 0054. The desk app host: the Apps sheet, one tap to add, and the sandboxed window

**Status:** Proposed (the UI/UX Engineer's half of launch row S-97; the Designer reads the sheet, Security reads the window's checks and policy)
**Date:** 2026-11-11
**Builds on:** the draft desk app contract (`draft-desk-app-contract.md`, sections 3, 5.6 and 6.1, and its Desk Engineer review, which wins where they differ), deskd `API.md` section 19, ADR 0053 (open tiles), ADR 0065 (tokens)

## Context
The owner wants "a plethora of useful apps" before launch (S-98), and the Bible "optional to add from the MA app marketplace" (S-150). Apps arrive as desk app contract packages. deskd's half is built and inert: `apps.list`, `apps.catalog.get`, `apps.install`, `apps.remove`, `apps.start`, `apps.view.get`, `apps.call`, `apps.mutate`, the `apps` topic and SUB `app:<id>:<topic>`. The Desk app had none of its half. The first app through it is the Bible, which deskd carries bundled in its own image until System's helper exists. Constraints: the office's own CSP (`script-src 'self'`, set by Platform's CDN), the 250 KB initial JS budget (246.7 KB on `main` before this), the house's ethos (fewer words, fewer decisions, no confirm cards), and no sign-in-age gates (C6).

## Decision
1. **The Apps sheet is the marketplace's first form**, one per desk, from the desk menu's Apps row, and on a phone (which has no desk menu) from an **Apps button that ends the carousel's thumb row**: a plus in a rounded square in the glyph tones (never the office's four squares), in a thumb-sized button, `aria-label` "Apps", shown wherever the menu's row would show. The contract's sample apps (`com.minimalistalliance.sample.*`, Tally) are a developer's: a production build drops them from what any desk lists, so a member never sees one. Its rows come from that desk's `apps.catalog.get` (deskd's checked copy; the Desk app never fetches or checks the catalog, C1): a 40 px glyph tile, the name, its one line, "Free", then Add to desk, or Open with a quiet Remove. A row opens its detail: the words, who made it, "Reviewed by Minimalist Alliance" (a bundled first-party app says "By Minimalist Alliance" instead), each permission in the contract's plain words or "It asks for nothing else: no internet, no files, no Browser.", the size, the same button, and the credits last. No ratings, counts or badges. Phones get it full screen.
2. **One tap to add.** Add to desk is `apps.install {id, version, packageSha256, grants}` with `grants` exactly that version's `permissions`, the ones the detail shows: no confirm card, no step-up (C6). The row says "Adding…" and nothing else moves; a refusal is one line on the row in plain words. A version deskd can't install yet (`installable: false`) shows why instead of the button.
3. **Remove keeps data** (contract decision 10): `apps.remove {id}`, no question asked, and adding the app again brings its data back. Deleting data is a later, separate control, if the owner wants one.
4. **Guild Desks:** only the Founder adds and removes (deskd refuses anyone else). A member's sheet lists what's installed, to open. The Apps row shows a member only once the Guild Desk has an app.
5. **Tiles.** An installed app is a tile, app id `app:<manifest id>` (the way an agent's is `agent:<id>`), placed at the first free spot when the desk says it's installed and taken off when it says it's gone, quietly, like the agent and Files tiles. Platform's layout document checks only `schemaVersion` and size (ma-platform `services/identity/src/layout.ts`), so it stores the new id as it is; the Desk app's own reader (`layoutDoc.ts`) accepts it. The widget is drawn by the Desk app from the app's `summary` with its own components in Konva: the first row as the title, the rest under it, the app's own problem line or the first row's time at the foot. A stopped app's tile says why in the contract's words ("Couldn't start", "Stopped: used too much memory" …), and its window offers Restart (`apps.start`).
6. **The glyph map.** Packages carry no icon yet. The Desk app keeps a small map of first-party ids to glyphs (the Bible's is the Designer's option 1 until the owner picks) and a generic app glyph for the rest. The lasting answer is an `icon` SVG in the package, hashed in the manifest and named in the catalog (Desk Engineer and Platform).
7. **The window checks before it builds.** `apps.view.get` gives the manifest and `ma-app.sig` exactly as signed and the view's three files. The review signature over the manifest's exact bytes must check with one of MA's public review keys shipped in the Desk app (key id per C3; Ed25519 or P-256 DER over SHA-256, both through WebCrypto; two keys at once; the lower tier of two), and each file's SHA-256 and size must be the manifest's, with no other file. **A bundled app is taken on its manifest's file hashes alone**, when deskd says `signatures: null, bundled: true` and its id is ours (`com.minimalistalliance.`): its files come from deskd's own image, and deskd is already what the Desk app trusts for everything on the desk. A revoked key or manifest the catalog lists is refused. Anything off: "This app couldn't be opened safely." and nothing is built. A frame that navigates away later is closed the same way.
8. **The frame.** `<iframe sandbox="allow-scripts" allow="clipboard-write">` from `srcdoc`, never `allow-same-origin`, with the view's own policy: `default-src 'none'`, scripts and styles only by their hashes, `img-src data:`, `font-src data:`, `form-action 'none'`, `base-uri 'none'`. A `srcdoc` frame inherits the office's own policy too, and the office's `script-src 'self'` blocks any inline script, so the view's script can't be inline: the document holds the files as data blocks, and a small boot script of ours, loaded by URL from the office's origin, runs the view's script from a `blob:` URL with its hash as `integrity` (CSP 3 matches a hash source against an external script's `integrity`), applies its style as a constructed stylesheet, and loads the office's UI font, Inter (`--ma-font-ui`, "Inter Variable"), from bytes (no `data:` URL needed). The view's own policy admits only that boot script and that hash, so the view can run no other code, `blob:` or not; `eval` is blocked. **The office's CSP needs `blob:` in `script-src`** for it (Platform's half). Without it the frame says "This app's window couldn't start." and the view never runs. **The clipboard is write only:** the frame's `allow` is `clipboard-write` alone, so a view's Copy (`navigator.clipboard.writeText`) works inside the member's own tap in the view, and reading the clipboard is refused, tap or not. This changes the contract's §3.2 ("no clipboard" becomes "write only"), for the Bible's Copy.
9. **The bridge** is one `MessagePort`, in the sample's protocol exactly (`docs/app-api/view-bridge.md`): the office posts `{t: "ma.bridge", appApi, tokens}` with the port; the view sends `call` and `sub`; the office answers `res` or `err`, sends events as `evt` and a theme switch as `theme`. A call goes to `apps.call` or `apps.mutate` by the manifest's `mutation` flag for that method, a mutation keeping the view's own `idem`; a subscription is SUB `app:<id>:<topic>`. Undeclared names, a mutation without a key and anything malformed are refused before deskd. The theme's tokens (ADR 0065, by their dotted names, plus `theme` and `font`, the Inter stack) go in at start and on every switch. **An app's own ink** is an `APP_INKS` entry keyed by its window's `data-app`, `app:<manifest id>` (the Bible's is the Designer's Lapis, pick 1): themes.css re-declares the inked properties on its window as it does for Files, and the host reads them from the frame's place in that window, so the view gets exactly what its window shows, and no ink is in the first load's JavaScript. Outside its window (an open tile, the phone's carousel) an app has the office's colors, as Files does.
10. **The frame budget** (contract 3.2): the office watches its own frames (Long Animation Frames, or rAF gaps where missing) only while a view shows. Three over 50 ms in 10 s while the office's own work is idle (the camera at rest, no input for a second) unload every view showing, with "Paused to keep your desk smooth" and Resume. A view's files are fetched and checked when the office is idle, once per version, so opening meets B4.
11. **An app's own keys in the office's shortcuts list:** a first-party app's keys (the Bible's ← →, `/`, `g`, Esc) are a group of their own, headed by its name, where it's installed on the desk in front (any desk while none is), from the host's first-party map until a package can say its own.
12. **Kill switches only, shipped on:** deskd's `/data/desk/apps-switch` (`available: false, reason: switched_off`) and client-config `deskApps: { enabled: false }`, read the way `openTiles` is (absent is on). Either hides the Apps row and leaves installed tiles as their widget with no window (an open tile included).
13. **Bundle.** The first load holds only the `app:` id kind (through layout, labels, glyphs, windows and proxies), the door (`deskapps/door.ts`: the host as a live binding, `host?.name(...)` and so on with the plain answer while it's null, and a count the host moves to redraw), a tiny check that loads the host's chunk when a desk says `available: true` or the layout holds an app tile (`door.ts` `noteApps`, `deskapps/watch.ts`), a plain tile until it's in, the window's first-load part (`deskapps/DeskAppWindow.tsx`) and the menu row. Everything else is the host's own lazy chunk (`deskapps/host/`): what each desk says (its store), the `apps` topic's handling, the widgets and their drawing, the sheet and its rules, the phone's Apps button, adding and removing, the checks, the frame, the bridge, the budget and the tiles' placing. It's +0.77 KB on `main` (247.43 KB initial of 250); the host chunk is 13.1 KB gzipped.

## Why
- One sheet per desk, from the desk's own catalog, keeps "nothing goes to the platform": the platform never learns which member browsed or added what (C1).
- One tap with no card is the owner's ethos; the detail shows everything a card would have, and an app that asks for more says so in that same place before the button.
- Checking in the Desk app as well as deskd means a compromised path between them can't put unreviewed code in front of the member; the bundled rule doesn't widen that, since a bundled app's bytes come from the deskd the Desk app already trusts.
- A boot script plus `blob:` is the only way a `srcdoc` view can run under the office's policy without `'unsafe-inline'` or `'unsafe-eval'` in it, and the view's own hash-only policy keeps the review's guarantee that what runs is what was signed.

## Consequences
- The office's CSP gains `blob:` in `script-src` (Platform). Code already running in the office could then run more from a `blob:` URL it made, which `eval` being blocked used to prevent; an injection with no script can't make a `blob:` URL. Security reads this.
- **The alternative, not built, for Security to pick.** The frame could load a dedicated same-origin page by `src` instead of `srcdoc`: `/_apps/frame.html` with its boot script `/_apps/boot.js`, still `sandbox="allow-scripts"` (so its origin stays opaque and it can't reach the office), served with a CSP header of its own for that path only: `default-src 'none'; script-src 'self' blob:; style-src 'none'; img-src data:; font-src data:; connect-src 'none'; form-action 'none'; base-uri 'none'; frame-ancestors 'self'`. The office's own `script-src` never gains `blob:`, so nothing changes for code in the office. The page gets the view's files over `postMessage` and runs them as today; since a header is the same for every view, the boot script adds the per-view pin itself (a `<meta>` policy with the view's script hash, added before the view runs; a second policy can only narrow). **What Platform changes:** for the built option, one line, `blob:` in `script-src` in Terraform's `local.desk_csp` (every env). For the alternative: `'self'` in the office's `frame-src` (today only the platform's host), a CloudFront cache behavior for `/_apps/*` with its own response headers policy carrying that page's CSP in place of the office's, and serving the two files from the Desk app's release bundle at a path that behavior matches (the Desk app's side: the page, `AppView` loading it by `src`, the files posted in).
- A view's Copy reaches the member's clipboard, only from a tap in the view; the frame can never read it.
- A view can still navigate its own frame to a host the office's `frame-src` admits (today the platform's host, for Account settings) with data in the URL: those reach only MA's own logs, and the Desk app closes such a frame at once. Narrowing `frame-src` to `/account`, or a wrapper frame with `frame-src 'none'`, would close it; for Security to choose.
- No production review key exists yet, so until Platform ships two, only a bundled app opens.
- Proven in Chromium only: a hash matching an external script's `integrity` is CSP 3, and other browsers may refuse the view (then it says it couldn't start).
