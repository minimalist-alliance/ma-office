# `m7g.large` on the real stack at 1920x1080 (TD-128, 2026-11-11)

Status: measured on one temporary staging test desk, `ma-desk-0.3.3`, `m7g.large` (2 vCPU Graviton3, 8 GiB, no CPU credits), kind `personal`, 1920x1080. Asked before Large and Extra large go on sale: the old-stack follow-up (`2026-11-follow-up.md`) ran `m7g.large` on Neko at a combined 1280x800 with a stand-in load, and had no 10-minute sessions and no simplified-view run. **No verdict on the size or the price:** that is the owner's. This says pass or fail against B11 to B13 (design §2.2, as `2026-11-real-stack.md` states them) and "Below 30 is unacceptable" (page fps).

## 2. How it was measured

- **The desk:** made by `scripts/desk-provision.sh --env staging --test --kind personal --size m7g.large` on `desk_image#current` (`ma-desk-0.3.3`) for the staging test account `desk-2`, tagged `Owner=syseng-td128`, us-east-1a. The provisioner took the size without complaint. `desk-rtc` 0.5.0, `desk-stream` 0.3.3 (`stream.conf` `08cd4cf8`: VFR, x264 `quantizer=23`, 8 Mbps cap, ultrafast, zerolatency, 2 threads), `deskd` `2026.11.10-ff58751`, Chromium `156.0.8078.79`. Screen, page and stream all 1920x1080, and every aimed click landed.
- **The client:** this workstation, as the brief says: Chrome for Testing 156.0.8078.4, new headless, software decode, 1920x1080 at scale 1, through the real Desk app on `office.staging.` (`2026.11.11-20c38353` for `measure`, `2026.11.11-bfe6c02d` for the sessions and the simplified view; staging moved between them). **RTT 49 to 66 ms (median 55 to 57), not the real-stack report's 30 ms from a box in us-east-1.** The workstation is shared by every session: its 1-minute load ran from 44 at the start to 5 at the end (column "Client load"). Page fps is counted inside the desk's Chromium, so the client's load doesn't reach it; latency and decoded fps are the client's and do carry it (round 3, at load 6 to 8, is the cleanest).
- **W3 kit** (`e2e/w3/`, its `README.md`): `measure` for 3 rounds with every scenario and the real-stack report's Big Buck Bunny clip (60 s from 2:00, 1080p60, no audio; the same video bytes as the 2026-11-09 runs, `4bafdddb…`); page fps from the rAF callback inside the desk's Chromium; input to screen by a key press through the app's window to the changed pixel in the decoded frame's callback (the real-stack report's callback method). Two specs added for this task:
  - `session.spec.ts`: the follow-up's 10-minute sessions through the real window. Browse: ten cycles, the odd ones on the scroll page (read 27 s, a 12 s wheel at 1200 px/s, read 19 s), the even ones on the static article with a page turned every 7 s; each starts with a navigation, which isn't scored. Video: the clip page for ten 56 s stretches. Page fps per second over the whole session, the client's `getStats` each second, the desk's CPU per cycle, the units' memory at the start and the end.
  - `simple.spec.ts`: the simplified view as a member gets it (client-config's switch left on): the address typed in the window's bar, the time from Enter to the view, `browser.minified.get`'s time on the desk, the desk's CPU, then 10 s of scrolling inside the view.
- **What this run can't say:** B12 at 30 ms RTT from an unloaded client (section 3); a GPU client, Safari, Firefox or a phone; real sites in the streamed view (the kit's pages only); loss or jitter on the path.
- **Raw files** (outside git: the JSON holds the desk's public address): `~/work/syseng-1109/td128/w3/td128-m7g.large-1920x1080/` (`numbers.md` and `numbers.json`, `session-m7g.json`, `simple-m7g.json`). `numbers.md` with the ids taken out: `image/evidence/m7g-large-2026-11-11/numbers.md`.

Facts beside it, with no reading of them:
- **The motion stress page is the one place the size shows.** Chromium runs it at 96 to 97% of one core with `desk-stream` at 50%, the machine at 77%: Chromium's software raster is the limit, as it was on `t4g.medium`, and `m7g.large` gives it about 37 fps against `t4g.medium`'s 22 to 24 and `c7g.large`'s 31.4 (an older stack). It is a stress page; no member workload in the kit is like it.
- **Idle latency reads higher here than on `t4g.medium` on Nov 9 (84 against 70 ms) and loaded latency lower (86 against 91).** The two runs differ in image (0.3.3 against 0.3.1), `desk-rtc` (0.5.0 against 0.4.1), app build and client load, and nothing here separates them, so this is not a size comparison. Round 3, with the client at load 6 to 7, gave 80.5 / 94.6 idle and 85.6 / 99.7 loaded.
- **B12 at 30 ms.** The real-stack report's wall-clock split puts the whole RTT inside the trip (15 ms up, 15 ms in the downstream at 30 ms). If that holds here, 25 ms less RTT would take about 25 ms off every latency above; that is an inference, not a measurement. Measured as it stands, every pass is under 90 / 150 at 55 ms by the callback, and the loaded p50 is over 90 once the one display frame is added (about 93).
- **The video page's stream is 7.1 to 7.2 Mbps** against 4.2 to 4.4 on `t4g.medium` at 0.3.1 with the same clip and `stream.conf`'s quantizer. Not investigated; the real-stack report saw the same split between `c7g.large` and `t4g.medium` (3.18 against 1.30 GB/h) and couldn't explain it either. The 10-minute video session agrees (3.21 GB/h).
- The page's `requestVideoFrameCallback` count on the video page is 48 to 49 a second while 60 frames a second are presented: that callback rate is Chromium's own and isn't the verdict (the same was seen on every desk).

## 5. The simplified view on real pages

`deskd` `2026.11.10-ff58751` serves section 14: `browser.minified.site.get` answered `ok` in 65 ms, and `/data/desk/minified-switch` was absent (on). Desk app `2026.11.11-bfe6c02d`, client-config's switch left on. Four pages in the order below, three rounds, each typed into the window's address bar:

| Page | Round 1 (first visit from this desk) | Rounds 2 and 3 | `browser.minified.get` on the desk | Desk CPU, Enter to 5 s after the view | Scrolling inside the view (client fps; desk CPU) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wikipedia, *Branch predictor* | **nothing in 30 s** (the first navigation of the new tab) | 423, 228 ms | 55 to 336 ms | 1.1 (nothing shown), 12.0 to 12.3% | 60.0 to 60.1; 3.6 to 3.7% |
| NPR's text-only front page | 2821 ms | 316, 324 ms | 54 to 102 ms | 2.9 to 4.2% | 60.0 to 60.1; 0.6 to 0.9% |
| DuckDuckGo's HTML search | 1039 ms | 735, 740 ms | 51 to 102 ms | 3.2 to 3.7% | 58.0 (p5 43.2, one 50 ms gap) to 60.1; 0.8 to 1.0% |
| Wikipedia, *Typography* | 1582 ms | 323, 317 ms | 54 to 385 ms | 12.0 to 16.7% | 60.0 to 60.1; 1.4 to 3.6% |

- **11 of 12 visits showed the view.** The one that didn't was the very first navigation of the tab the run opened; the window had no view element at all for 30 s and the app sent no `browser.minified.get` for it. The same page showed the view in both later rounds. One case, not chased.
- While the view showed, the stream was `warm` (`deskd`'s `/health`) and the desk ran at 0.6 to 3.7% while the member scrolled, against 46.5% for the streamed scroll page. The client draws the view, so its scrolling frame rate is this workstation's (58.0 to 60.1).
- The app also called `browser.minified.image` and `browser.minified.act` on its own during the run; the run clicked nothing on any page.

## 6. Cost

From AWS's price list (`aws pricing get-products`, us-east-1, on-demand Linux, shared tenancy, read 2026-11-11): `m7g.large` $0.0816 an hour, `c7g.large` $0.0725, `t4g.medium` $0.0336; gp3 $0.08 a GB-month; a public IPv4 address $0.005 an hour. A month is 730 hours.

| Basis | `m7g.large` | `c7g.large` | `t4g.medium` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instance only | $59.57 | $52.93 | $24.53 |
| ADR 0033's basis (instance and 20 GB gp3, which is what its $54.52 and $26.13 are) | **$61.17** | $54.52 | $26.13 |
| As this desk was provisioned (12 GB root and 32 GB data gp3, one public IPv4) | **$66.74** | $60.10 | $31.70 |

`m7g.large` and `c7g.large` have no CPU credits, so these are flat whatever the member does; `t4g.medium` adds surplus credits above 24 an hour (`2026-11-alliance-desk.md` section 5). The follow-up's "about $61 (estimate)" is confirmed on ADR 0033's basis. `m7g.large` is $6.64 a month more than `c7g.large` for the same two Graviton3 vCPUs and twice the memory (8 against 4 GiB).

## 7. Spend and clean-up

About $0.2, estimated and not read from a bill: the `m7g.large` for 56 minutes (09:23:44 to 10:19:27 UTC, $0.08), 44 GB of gp3 and its address for the hour (about $0.01), and about 1.2 GB of stream to this workstation (about $0.1). Ended with `scripts/test-desk-stop.sh staging --desk <its id>` (marked `stopping`), then `terminate-instances`, a delete of its data volume (the provisioner keeps it on termination), its IAM role and instance profile, and its row in `ma-staging-desks`. **Checked by tag `Owner=syseng-td128` and `DeskId`: only the terminated instance lists; no volume, key pair or security group.** The shared test desk, the owner's desk and every other desk were not touched.

## 8. What stayed unmeasured

- **B12 from a client in us-east-1 at 30 ms RTT with no load.** This was 55 ms and a shared workstation.
- **Real sites in the streamed view** (bot walls from AWS addresses), video calls, WebGL; the motion page is the only stress case.
- **Many sessions or a day of use:** one 10-minute browse and one 10-minute video session; memory grew 380 to 496 MB in Chromium over them, and nothing says where it settles.
- **The size of the video page's stream** (7.1 Mbps here against 4.2 on `t4g.medium`), and why the first navigation of a new tab got no simplified view.
- **Off to live under 300 ms:** 373 to 437 ms through the window, which includes `desk-stream`'s start; whether B13 counts that is the reading the real-stack report left open.
